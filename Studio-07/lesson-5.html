STUDIO 7 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have proportion, symbolism, and setting. Now commit to a plan and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · LOCK YOUR PLAN

Your Studio Project is The Symbolic Self: a portrait, most likely a self-portrait, that uses objects, setting, and symbol to tell its sitter's story, not just their face. You already tested your symbols and sketched a thumbnail in Lesson 4. Now make it official.

✅ A FINISHED PLAN ANSWERS FOUR QUESTIONS
Who is the sitter (yourself, unless your teacher approves another willing subject)? What two or three objects will appear, and what does each one mean? What setting will surround them, and why that one? What pose and expression fit the story you are telling? Write short answers to all four before you touch your final surface.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which strategy from Lesson 2 is closest to what you are planning?
🖌️ PART 2 · BUILD IN ORDER
  1. Set your reference. Take a clear reference photo of yourself in your chosen pose (a mirror selfie under good light works), or arrange your willing subject. Gather your chosen symbolic objects nearby so you can look at them directly too.
  2. Block in the head using your thirds map (Lesson 3). Lightly place the head shape, the eye line, and the landmark guides before any detail.
  3. Place your objects and setting (Lesson 4). Position each symbolic object and the setting elements according to your thumbnail. Check that nothing crowds the face out.
  4. Render the face. Work from your reference, refining proportion as you go. Do not rush past this step; likeness is still your foundation.
  5. Render the symbols and setting with equal care. An object rendered sloppily reads as an afterthought. Give your symbols the same attention you gave the face.
💡 MEDIA CHOICE IS OPEN
Graphite, colored pencil, paint, collage elements, or digital tools are all acceptable, as long as the final piece is a two-dimensional portrait. Choose the medium that lets you render your specific symbols and setting most convincingly.

🥽 VR Session · Staging the Symbolic Self

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: Staging the Symbolic Self.

  • 1Before you enter: have your locked plan (sitter, objects, setting, pose) ready. Enter your VR space and choose any Location you like that fits, or roughly matches, the setting you planned.
  • 2Stage (about 10 min): using the space's props or your imagination, arrange your three chosen symbolic objects around where your figure will stand or sit. Try more than one arrangement.
  • 3Critique (about 8 min): critique your own staged arrangement using the describe, analyze, interpret, judge order. Does the interpret step land on the meaning you intended, or on something else? Adjust if not.
  • 4Reflect (about 5 min): answer aloud or in writing: "What did staging this in 3D space show me that my flat thumbnail did not?" In a live session, share and respond to one classmate.
  • 5Exit: capture a screenshot of your staged arrangement and upload it. No screenshot tool? Write two or three sentences describing your final arrangement.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND SYMBOLIC PORTRAITURE
An AI image generator can produce a technically polished portrait of "a teenager holding a sewing needle" in seconds. What it cannot do is know that the needle was your grandmother's, or that she taught you to sew the year she lived with you. The meaning in a symbolic self-portrait comes from a true, specific fact about the actual sitter, something no generator has access to. It also cannot render the exact object sitting on your desk from a photo you took yourself. The choosing, the remembering, and the drawing are the work being graded here, and they must be yours.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A locked plan protects your two weeks of work. Once you know your sitter, your symbols, and your setting, and can explain why each belongs, the rest of the Studio is execution: proportion, patient rendering, and equal care for the face and everything around it.
