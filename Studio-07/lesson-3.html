STUDIO 7 · LESSON 3
Facial Proportion
Before a face can carry meaning, it has to read as believable. Grab a pencil and a mirror or a reference photo. ~50 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Map the head into thirds to place the eyes, nose, and mouth in believable positions, and use simple width guides to space the eyes and mouth correctly. This is the technical floor from Lesson 1: the proportion grid that everything symbolic in this Studio gets built on top of.
📚 PART 1 · THE HEAD IN THIRDS

Most adult faces, viewed straight on, divide into three roughly equal horizontal bands. This is not a rigid rule that erases individual variation, it is a starting map that catches the most common beginner mistakes: eyes placed too high, a nose that is too short, or a mouth crowded up against the chin.

💡 FOUR LANDMARKS, THREE EQUAL GAPS
Mark the hairline, the brow line, the base of the nose, and the chin. The three gaps between them should look roughly equal. The eyes sit at the exact midline of the whole head, not up near the hairline, which is the single most common beginner error. The mouth sits about a third of the way down from the nose base to the chin, not centered between them.
✍️ PART 2 · GUIDED PRACTICE (do this now)

Use a mirror, a photo of your own face, or a photo of a willing family member. Work lightly in pencil so your guide lines can be erased later.

  1. Draw the head shape (5 min). A simple oval or egg shape, roughly two thirds as wide as it is tall for a front-facing view. Do not add features yet.
  2. Mark the thirds (5 min). Lightly draw the hairline, brow, nose-base, and chin lines. Check that the three gaps look equal before moving on.
  3. Place the eye line (3 min). Draw one line at the exact vertical midpoint of the head. The eyes go here, not higher.
  4. Space the eyes (5 min). The two eyes and the gap between them should be roughly three equal eye-widths across the face. Draw both eyes on the eye line.
  5. Add nose and mouth (7 min). Nose base sits on your third guide line. Mouth sits about a third of the way from the nose base down to the chin, and its width lines up roughly with the inner edge of each eye.
  6. Check against your reference. Compare your map to the real face in front of you. Every real face varies from the map slightly. Note where yours (or your reference's) differs, and adjust.
⚡ TRY IT · SPOT THE PROPORTION ERROR
Each description is a common beginner mistake. Identify what is wrong.
A drawn face has eyes placed just below the hairline, leaving a huge blank forehead below them all the way to the nose.
A drawn face has the mouth exactly centered between the nose and the chin, making the chin look very short.
🖉️ SKILL BUILDER SKETCH (turn in this week)

Facial Proportion Map

Draw one front-facing head using the thirds method: mark the hairline, brow, nose base, and chin, place the eye line at the midpoint, space the eyes correctly, and add nose and mouth at their guide positions. Leave your guide lines visible and lightly labeled so your process shows. This is a completion-graded practice rung, not a finished portrait.

Full directions and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder A: Facial Proportion Map. Photograph your page and upload it, then save a copy to your OneDrive portfolio folder.

📝 VOCABULARY
Proportion (facial)The size and position relationships between facial features and the head as a whole.
Thirds mapA guide dividing the front-facing head into three roughly equal horizontal bands.
Eye lineThe horizontal guide at the vertical midpoint of the head, where the eyes are placed.
LandmarkA fixed reference point on the face (hairline, brow, nose base, chin) used to check proportion.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A proportion map is not a cage, it is a foundation. Once eyes, nose, and mouth sit in believable places, you have earned the freedom to spend your attention on what actually makes this Studio's project matter: the symbols, the setting, and the story.
