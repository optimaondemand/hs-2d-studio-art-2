STUDIO 8 · LESSON 1
Look Back at Your Body of Work
No new artist this Studio. The subject is seven Studios of your own work. ~45 min
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
What does your work so far reveal about the artist you are becoming?
You have finished seven Studios: observation, composition, color, space, pattern and print, collage, and identity. Each one asked something different of you. This Studio steps back from making new work to look at all of it together, choose what deserves revision, and build the first real portfolio of your year.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

Picture a wall, not a folder buried on a drive: an actual gallery wall, with every Studio you have completed hanging on it in order. Look at the wall below and imagine your own seven pieces filling those first frames.

Working artists do this on purpose: step back from the easel, look at a stretch of finished work as a set, and ask what it says as a whole rather than piece by piece. That eighth frame, the question mark, is what you build this Studio: not a ninth new artwork, but a curated argument built from the seven you already have.

💬 WONDER FIRST
Look at your imagined wall again. If a stranger walked past it and had thirty seconds, what is the one thing you would want them to notice about how you have grown from Studio 1 to Studio 7? Hold that answer. You will build your entire portfolio and artist statement around it.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Be honest: have you looked at your Studios 1 through 7 together, side by side, since you finished them?
📚 PART 2 · A PORTFOLIO IS AN ARGUMENT

A common mistake is to think a portfolio is just a pile: everything you made, in the order you made it. A stronger portfolio is a curated argument: choosing a smaller set of works on purpose, then arranging them so a viewer understands your growth without you standing there explaining it.

This Studio, your argument is simple: here is what I have learned so far, and here is proof that I can revise, not just produce. Two ideas carry that argument: revision and curation. You will practice both this week before building the real thing next week.

PortfolioA curated, purposeful selection of an artist's work, not a complete archive of everything made.
CurationChoosing which works to include and in what order, so the set makes a clear statement together.
RevisionReturning to a finished piece to genuinely improve it, guided by specific critique.
Body of workEverything an artist has made over a period of time, considered as a whole.
▶  In your notebook, list the titles of your seven Studio pieces from memory before you open this.
Most students can name three or four instantly and have to think hard about the rest. That gap between "I made it" and "I can call it to mind clearly" is worth noticing. The piece you struggled to remember may be exactly the one that needs a second look this Studio.
🔍 PART 3 · WHY THIS STUDIO EXISTS

Every other Studio this year has moved forward: a new technique, a new artist, a new project. Studio 8 moves sideways: a checkpoint built in on purpose, at the halfway point, because artists who never pause to review their own growth tend to repeat the same habits without noticing.

Your project for these two weeks is the Mid-Year Portfolio: you will revise one or two earlier pieces, select and sequence four to six works from Studios 1 through 7 into a curated set, and write a reflective artist statement about your growth. No new subject matter, no new artist study. The material is entirely your own.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Artists revise. A portfolio is a curated argument about growth, not a pile of everything. For the next two weeks you look backward on purpose, so the rest of the year can build forward on solid ground.
