STUDIO 8 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
Turn your revision plan and sequence into an actual mid-year portfolio, starting with a gallery of your own work in VR. ~50 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · ACT ON YOUR REVISION PLAN

Your Studio Project is the Mid-Year Portfolio: revise one or two pieces from Studios 1–7, curate four to six pieces into a sequenced set, and write a reflective artist statement about your growth. You already have the two hardest decisions made, your Lesson 3 revision plan and your Lesson 4 sequence and captures; this week you carry them out.

🏷️ A STRONG MID-YEAR PORTFOLIO HAS THREE TRAITS
It shows at least one genuine revision, not a redo, guided by a specific critique finding. It is curated, four to six pieces chosen on purpose rather than all seven included by default. And it is sequenced with intent, in an order that tells a clear story about your growth rather than a random or purely chronological stack.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which part of the Mid-Year Portfolio are you most confident about right now?
🖌️ PART 2 · THIS WEEK'S BUILD ORDER

Work in this order so revising, the harder task, happens while you still have the most time and energy left in the cycle.

  1. Revise first (most of your studio time). Make the one or two targeted changes from your Lesson 3 plan. Stop at your planned stopping point; do not let a revision drift into a full redo.
  2. Re-photograph or re-scan the revised piece(s). A revision is not finished until it is captured as cleanly as the rest of your set, following Lesson 4's straight-on, even-light standard.
  3. Lock your final sequence. Confirm your four to six pieces, in order, using your Lesson 4 plan as the starting point; adjust if the revised piece changes the story.
  4. Draft your artist statement (see Lesson 6). You will finish this next lesson, but jotting early notes now, while the revision is fresh, makes it easier to write honestly later.
💡 IF A REVISION IS NOT WORKING
If your planned change is not improving the piece, that is useful information, not a failure. Note what you tried and why it did not help; you can say so honestly in your artist statement. A student who tried a real revision and can explain what they learned earns more credit than one who avoided revising altogether.

🥽 VR Session · Hang Your Own Mid-Year Gallery

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: Mid-Year Gallery.

  • 1Before you enter: have photos of your seven Studio pieces (or your shortlisted four to six) ready to reference or bring in. Enter your VR space; you may choose any Location you like.
  • 2Hang your gallery (about 12 min): using the VR drawing, image, or building tool, arrange your own works along a wall in your planned sequence, exactly as you would hang a real show.
  • 3Walk it and self-critique (about 8 min): step back and walk your own gallery wall the way a visitor would. Does the sequence read the way you intended? Does one piece feel out of place, or does the order actually tell the story you meant?
  • 4Exit: capture a screenshot of your hung gallery, or write two or three sentences describing what you noticed walking your own sequence, and upload it.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND YOUR PORTFOLIO
An AI tool can suggest a plausible order for a set of images, or generate generic text describing "growth as an artist" in confident-sounding sentences. What it cannot do is know what you actually struggled with, revised, or learned across Studios 1 through 7, because it was not there and has not seen your work develop. The sequence, the revision, and the reflective statement in this Studio have to come from your own honest look at your own body of work; an AI-written statement about growth you did not actually experience is not a portfolio, it is a guess.
