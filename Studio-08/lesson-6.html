STUDIO 8 · LESSON 6
Refine, Reflect & Present
Finalize your curated set, write the statement that ties it together, and submit. ~50 min + studio time
🔄 PART 1 · LOOK AT YOUR FINAL SIX AS A SET

Before you submit, look at your finished, sequenced portfolio the way a stranger will: start to finish, no explanation from you standing beside it. Does it read as a clear, connected story about growth, or as a random stack of unrelated pieces? This is your last chance to reorder, swap a piece, or adjust for something your revision changed.

Studio 2 (revised, ends the set)
👀 LOOK AGAIN
Notice this example keeps only six of seven Studios, marks its two revised pieces, and deliberately ends on a revised, strong piece rather than in strict date order. Your own set does not need to match this exact pattern, but it should be able to explain a reason, in one sentence, for every choice: what stayed, what was cut, what was revised, and what comes last.
🔍 PART 2 · ONE LAST CRITIQUE, ON THE FINISHED SET

Run the critique process one final time, on the finished, sequenced portfolio itself, not the individual pieces.

D · Describe

How many pieces are in your final set, in what order, and which are revised? State it plainly.

A · Analyze

What connects the pieces (a medium, a subject, a growth arc)? Does the sequence support that connection, or fight it?

I · Interpret

If a stranger saw only this set, what would they conclude about the artist you are becoming? Is that the impression you want to leave?

J · Judge

Is this set ready to submit, or does one more small adjustment, a swap, a reorder, a second capture, make it noticeably stronger? Make that adjustment now if so.

💭 FINAL CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Looking at your finished set now, which is closest to true?
📝 PART 3 · WRITE YOUR REFLECTIVE ARTIST STATEMENT

This statement travels with your portfolio. It is two or three focused sentences, in your own voice, reflecting on your growth across Studios 1 through 7. Type it into the submission text box with your set.

A strong statement answers three things:

  • What does your curated set, read start to finish, reveal about how you have grown since Studio 1?
  • What did you revise, and what specifically did that revision teach you about your own habits as an artist?
  • What is one thing you want to carry forward, on purpose, into Studios 9 through 18?
▶  Draft your three sentences in your notebook first, then open this for a sample of the register (do not copy it).
"Looking at my seven pieces together, I noticed I almost always play it safe with value, keeping everything in a narrow middle range, so for this portfolio I revised my Studio 6 collage to push its shadows much darker. That one change taught me the flatness was not the paper's fault or the subject's fault; it was a habit I could simply decide to break. Going forward, I want to check my value range on purpose in every piece, not just when a critique forces me to notice it." Your statement should be this specific, but about your own set and your own revision.

✍️ DO NOT FORGET
Every finished artwork carries its artist's name, including revised pieces. Confirm your revised piece is still signed after the change. A signature is how you say: this is mine, I made it, I stand behind it, even after I went back and made it better.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

You began this Studio picturing an empty eighth frame on a wall of your own work. You are filling it now, not with a new piece, but with proof that you can look honestly at what you have made, change it on purpose, and say clearly what you learned. That is the argument a portfolio makes, and it is the argument the rest of your year builds on.
