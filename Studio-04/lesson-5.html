STUDIO 4 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the tools. Now choose your space, plan its depth, and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE AN ENVIRONMENT

Your Studio Project is Step Into This Place: an original environment, interior or exterior, real or imagined, drawn so that a viewer feels they could walk into it. You will use one-point perspective for the built structure and the no-line cues to deepen everything else. Any media that lets you control value works: graphite, ink, colored pencil, marker, or a digital drawing app.

🏷️ STRONG SPACES SHARE THREE TRAITS
They have a clear path for the eye into the distance (a road, a hall, a canal, a row of anything), they mix near and far so the depth cues have something to do, and they mean something to you: a place you know, a place you wish existed, or a place that tells a small story. Avoid a single flat wall with nothing receding; give your space somewhere to go.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which kind of space are you leaning toward?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN THE DEPTH BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Canaletto planned his perspective before he painted; so will you. A few minutes of setup on scrap paper prevents a warped space on your good sheet.

  1. Decide your viewpoint. Where does the viewer stand, and how high are their eyes? That choice places your horizon line. Low horizon looks up; high horizon looks down into the space.
  2. Set the perspective skeleton. On scrap, rule your horizon and mark your vanishing point. Lightly draw the main orthogonals of the biggest structure (the walls, the road, the row) so the whole space agrees on one point.
  3. Thumbnail two or three versions (about 10 min). Try the vanishing point in different places, or a higher and lower horizon. Pick the composition that pulls the eye in most strongly.
  4. Transfer and layer the cues. Block the chosen structure onto your good paper with the perspective lines. Then add foreground, middle-ground, and background elements, using overlap, size, and atmospheric fade so near reads near and far reads far.
💡 SAVE ATMOSPHERE FOR LAST
Build the whole space at full clarity first, then push the distance back at the end: lighten the far values, soften the far edges, and drop the far detail. It is much easier to fade a finished background than to guess the fade while you are still drawing the front.

🥽 VR Session · Walk the Vanishing Point

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: Walk the Vanishing Point.

  • 1Before you enter: have your perspective vocabulary ready (horizon line, vanishing point, orthogonal). Enter your VR space and choose any Location that has a long straight view: a corridor, a colonnade, a street, a hall.
  • 2Find the point (about 8 min): stand so the space runs straight away from you. Notice where all the receding edges seem to meet. Use your VR note tool to mark that spot as the vanishing point, and place a note at your own eye level for the horizon line.
  • 3Trace the orthogonals (about 9 min): use the VR drawing tool to trace three or four receding edges (floor seams, ceiling lines, the tops of a row of objects) back toward your marked point. Watch them converge.
  • 4Reflect (about 8 min): answer aloud or in writing: "Standing inside a real space, what did tracing the orthogonals show me that a flat drawing did not?" If you are in a live session, share and respond to one classmate.
  • 5Exit: capture a screenshot of your marked point and traced lines and upload it. No screenshot tool? Write two or three sentences describing what you found.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND PERSPECTIVE
An AI image generator can produce a picture that looks like a deep space in seconds, and it will often look convincing at a glance. It also frequently gets perspective subtly wrong: orthogonals that do not agree on one point, stairs that do not connect, edges that drift. The reason is that the generator imitates the look of depth without following the geometry underneath it. This project is teaching you that geometry, the thing the machine skips. The perspective decisions, the construction, and the drawing are what is being graded here, and they must be yours.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Artists decide the space before they commit. A clear viewpoint, one agreed vanishing point, and a plan for near-to-far prevent hours of warped lines. Set your horizon, aim your orthogonals, and let the depth grow from the cues, not from luck.
