STUDIO 2 · LESSON 4
Balance & Movement
Distribute the weight across the frame, then build a path for the eye to travel. Keep pencil and paper close. ~55 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Distribute visual weight with symmetrical and asymmetrical balance, and design movement, the path the eye follows, using leading lines and placement. Focal point (Lesson 3) decides where the eye starts; balance and movement decide how the whole frame holds together and where the eye travels next.
⚖️ PART 1 · BALANCE IS VISUAL WEIGHT

Balance is how the visual weight of a composition is spread across the frame. Large, dark, detailed, or high-contrast things feel "heavy"; small, light, or plain things feel "light." A balanced picture feels settled; an unbalanced one feels like it might tip. There are two main kinds.

💡 ASYMMETRY IS NOT IMBALANCE
Asymmetrical does not mean lopsided. It means the two sides differ but still weigh about the same to the eye. One big object can be balanced by a few small ones, or by a large area of empty space. Asymmetry usually feels more alive than perfect symmetry, which is why most artists reach for it.
🔄 PART 2 · MOVEMENT IS THE EYE'S PATH

Movement is the route the artist builds for the viewer's eye. A strong composition does not just have a focal point; it sends the eye on a journey and, ideally, keeps it circulating inside the frame instead of sliding off the edge. Leading lines, overlapping objects, and the direction things point all steer that path.

▶  In your notebook, sketch three objects and draw the eye path you would want a viewer to take, before you open this. Then check your thinking.
A strong eye path usually enters at the focal point, moves through the supporting objects along a line or curve, and loops back inward rather than running off the edge. If your arrow shoots straight out of the frame, add an object or a line near that edge to turn the eye back in. There is no single right path; what matters is that you designed one on purpose.

📝 VOCABULARY
BalanceHow visual weight is distributed across the frame.
Symmetrical balanceEqual, mirrored weight on both sides; formal and calm.
Asymmetrical balanceDifferent content on each side that still weighs the same; dynamic.
Visual weightHow much a shape pulls the eye, based on size, value, and detail.
MovementThe path the artist builds for the eye to follow.
Leading linesLines that steer the eye along that path toward the focal point.
