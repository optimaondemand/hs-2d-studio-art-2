STUDIO 2 · LESSON 6
Refine, Reflect & Present
Great compositions are finished, not abandoned. Critique your own arrangements, refine them, and present them well. ~55 min + studio time
🔄 PART 1 · PROVE YOUR GROWTH

Remember the careless arrangement from Lesson 1, the one you dropped on the page with no plan? Take it out now and set it beside your three finished arrangements. This is your two-week before and after.

👀 LOOK AT THEM SIDE BY SIDE
What is different? Almost always: the finished pieces have a clear focal point, the weight is balanced across the frame, and the eye has a designed path to follow. The careless one has none of that; the eye wanders. That difference is not about the objects, which never changed. It is entirely composition. Keep all four in your portfolio; they are evidence of what your eye learned to control.
🔍 PART 2 · CRITIQUE YOUR OWN WORK

Before you call them done, critique your own arrangements using the same four steps you used on Hopper in Lesson 2: describe, analyze, interpret, judge. Be your own first critic. This is where good compositions become finished ones.

D · Describe

Step back from each drawing. Where does your eye actually land first? Where does it go next? Trace the path out loud.

A · Analyze

Check your tools. Is there one clear focal point, or does the eye have nowhere to settle? Is the weight balanced, or does the frame feel like it tips the wrong way? Do your leading lines actually lead somewhere?

I · Interpret

Does each arrangement actually communicate its mood? Would a stranger read one as calm, one as tense, and one as isolated, without being told?

J · Judge

Which arrangement is weakest, and what one change would most improve it? Usually it is moving the focal point off center or opening up empty space. Make that change before you submit.

💭 REVISION CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
You look at one drawing and your eye keeps sliding off the right edge and out of the frame. What is the most likely fix?
📝 PART 3 · WRITE YOUR ARTIST STATEMENT

Your artist statement travels with the work. It is three or four focused sentences, in your own voice, that show you arranged on purpose. Type it into the submission text box with your drawings.

A strong statement answers three things:

  • What objects did you choose, and what three feelings did you assign to your three arrangements?
  • Which composition tools did you use to build each feeling, and where? (Name at least two: focal point, rule of thirds, balance, leading lines, movement, empty space.)
  • Which arrangement changed the feeling most, and what surprised you about doing it with the same objects?
▶  Draft your sentences in your notebook first, then open this for a sample of the register (do not copy it).
"I used a mug, a spoon, and a folded cloth for all three drawings. For calm I grouped them low and level on a strong horizontal; for tension I tilted them and crowded them into one corner on a diagonal; for isolation I placed the mug alone, small, far to the right, surrounded by empty space. The isolation version changed the feeling the most, and I was surprised that leaving the page mostly empty said more than filling it." Your statement should be this specific, but about your objects and your arrangements.

✍️ DO NOT FORGET
Every finished artwork carries its artist's name. Sign your drawings. A signature is how you say: this is mine, I made it, I stand behind it.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

You began Studio 2 dropping objects on a page with no plan. Two weeks later you can arrange the same objects three ways and change how each one feels. That control over the viewer's eye is composition, and it now joins accurate seeing as a foundation for everything else this course will ask you to make.
