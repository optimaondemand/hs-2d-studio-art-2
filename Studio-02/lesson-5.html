STUDIO 2 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the tools. Now choose your objects, plan three arrangements, and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE ONE SET OF OBJECTS

Your Studio Project is Three Arrangements: three drawings of the same small group of objects, where the only thing that changes between them is the composition. Each version should communicate a different feeling. The suggested three are calm, tension, and isolation. Because the objects stay the same, every difference the viewer sees will come from your arrangement.

🏷️ GOOD OBJECT SETS SHARE THREE TRAITS
Pick two or three simple objects (a mug, a spoon, a folded cloth; or a book, glasses, and a key). They should be easy to rearrange and varied in size or shape so you have something to balance. Keep them plain: this project is about arrangement, not rendering a complicated surface.

Here is how the same objects can carry three different moods through arrangement alone.

💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which mood are you most drawn to build first?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Composers plan on paper first. You will thumbnail each arrangement before committing it to a finished drawing, so you solve the arrangement while it is still cheap to change.

  1. Set your objects and light. Place your two or three objects on a plain surface under one steady light. Keep the same objects for all three drawings.
  2. Thumbnail each mood (about 12 min). Make small, fast thumbnails for calm, tension, and isolation. Do not detail them. For each, mark the focal point and sketch the eye path. Use the rule of thirds, balance, and leading lines from Lessons 3 and 4.
  3. Choose and enlarge. Pick your strongest thumbnail for each mood and lightly block the arrangement onto three sheets (or three areas of one sheet).
  4. Draw all three. Render each arrangement carefully in graphite, charcoal, or mixed media, using the observation skills from Studio 1. The rendering can be similar across all three; the arrangement is what must differ.
💡 CHANGE ARRANGEMENT, NOT OBJECTS
The whole point is proving that composition alone changes meaning. Do not swap objects, add new ones, or change the lighting between versions. Keep everything the same except placement, size in the frame, spacing, and the eye path. That discipline is what makes the project work.

🥽 VR Session · The Composition Table

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: The Composition Table.

  • 1Before you enter: have your composition vocabulary and the four critique steps ready. Enter your VR space and load the Studio 2 gallery snapshot from Canvas. You may choose any gallery Location you like.
  • 2Arrange (about 10 min): gather three virtual objects on a table or surface. Compose them three ways: calm, then tension, then isolation. Capture each arrangement. Change only placement, not the objects.
  • 3Critique (about 8 min): move close to one gallery work and place short notes running describe, analyze, interpret, judge on its composition. Which arrangement tools (focal point, balance, movement) did the artist use?
  • 4Reflect: answer aloud or in writing: "Which of your three arrangements changed the feeling the most, and why?" In a live session, share and respond to one classmate.
  • 5Exit: capture screenshots of your three arrangements and upload them. No screenshot tool? Write two or three sentences describing what you arranged and why.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND COMPOSITION
An AI image generator can produce a well-composed picture on request, and it will often default to common rules like the rule of thirds. What it cannot do is make the specific compositional decisions this project asks of you: arranging your objects, choosing your focal point, and controlling the eye path to communicate calm, tension, or isolation on purpose. The skill being graded here is your ability to decide where things go and why. Those arrangement choices, and the drawings, must be yours.
🎨 STUDIO PROJECT (the graded artwork for this Studio)

Three Arrangements

Three drawings of the same small set of objects, each arranged to communicate a different feeling (calm, tension, isolation) using focal point, balance, and movement. Graphite, charcoal, or mixed media. This is the one teacher-graded assessment of Studio 2, submitted with a short artist statement.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Three drawings, one set of objects, three feelings. Thumbnail first so the arrangement is solved before you commit, hold everything but placement constant, and let composition prove that where things go is a decision with real power.
