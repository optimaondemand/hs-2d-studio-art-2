STUDIO 12 · LESSON 1
A Painting of Nothing
Every Studio so far has asked you to record something real. This one asks what happens when you let go of the subject entirely. ~45 min
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
Can a painting of nothing recognizable still be about something?
Every Studio Project you have made until now had a subject you could name: a cup, a room, a face, a poster's message. In this Studio you will make an artwork with no subject at all, only line, shape, and color, and it will still say something real. It starts with a painter who believed color and form could carry meaning on their own.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

Spend a full minute with the painting below before reading anything else. Do not search for a hidden object. There is none to find. Just follow where your eye travels, and notice what you feel as it moves.

There is no cup here, no face, no landscape. There are only shapes, colors, and the relationships between them, and yet the painting is anything but empty. Circles nest inside circles, triangles cut across curves, colors push and pull against each other. Kandinsky built this the way a composer builds a piece of music: not by depicting a subject, but by arranging pure elements so that they move you.

💬 WONDER FIRST
If nothing here is a "thing," what is it that you are actually reacting to? Hold your answer. This Studio is built to help you answer it with your own hands, not just your eyes.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Be honest: what was your first reaction to a painting with no recognizable subject?
📚 PART 2 · NAME WHAT YOU SEE

Art that has no recognizable subject goes by two related names, and the difference between them matters. Abstract art starts from something real and simplifies, distorts, or fragments it until it is barely recognizable, or not recognizable at all, but a trace of the original subject remains in how it was built. Non-objective art (also called non-representational) never started from a subject in the first place. It is built entirely from the elements of art themselves: line, shape, color, value, texture, space.

Kandinsky is often credited as one of the first painters to commit fully to non-objective work. He believed color and form could function like music: a violin does not need to depict anything to move a listener, and Kandinsky believed a painting should not need a subject to move a viewer either.

🎨 ABSTRACT (starts from something real)

  • Begins with an observed subject
  • Simplifies, distorts, or fragments it
  • A trace of the original often remains
  • You may still sense "a figure" or "a landscape"

⚫ NON-OBJECTIVE (starts from nothing recognizable)

  • No observed subject at any stage
  • Built purely from line, shape, color, space
  • Meaning comes from relationships, not depiction
  • Closer to how music works than how photography works
THE ONE IDEA OF THIS STUDIO
You do not need a subject
to say something true.
Line, shape, and color can carry an idea or a feeling entirely on their own. The rest of this Studio teaches you how.

Two vocabulary words will do a lot of work across this Studio. Meet them now.

AbstractArt that starts from a real subject and simplifies or distorts it, keeping a trace of the original.
Non-objectiveArt with no subject at any stage, built entirely from the elements of art.
🔍 PART 3 · WHY THIS COMES NOW

In Studio 9 you learned to abstract from observation, stylizing a real object step by step. This Studio goes further: no observed object at all, so you no longer have a subject to fall back on when a choice feels uncertain. Every decision about line, shape, and color has to justify itself.

Your project for these two weeks is called An Idea Without a Subject. You will choose one idea or emotion, something like tension, calm, chaos, or hope, and express it using only the elements of art: no recognizable object anywhere in the finished work.

▶  Before you read on, name one emotion in your notebook that you think would be hard to draw as a "thing." Then open this.
Most people pick something like anxiety, calm, or excitement, exactly because those feelings do not have an obvious object. That is the gap this Studio closes. By Lesson 5 you will have specific tools (color temperature, line quality, balance, rhythm) for building that feeling without ever drawing a "thing" at all.

Non-objective art expresses idea and emotion through pure elements: line, shape, color, no subject required. Kandinsky trusted color and form to move a viewer the way music does. For the next two weeks, you will test whether you can trust them too.
