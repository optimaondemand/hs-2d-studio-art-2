STUDIO 12 · LESSON 6
Refine, Reflect & Present
Non-objective work is finished, not abandoned. Critique your own choices, refine them, and present the work well. ~55 min + studio time
🔄 PART 1 · PROVE YOUR GROWTH

Remember the feeling scribble from Lesson 1, the two-minute unplanned mark-making you made before you learned anything? Set it beside your Studio Project now. This is your two-week before and after.

👀 LOOK AT THE TWO SIDE BY SIDE
What is different? Almost always: the finished piece has actual rhythm instead of random marks, a considered balance instead of an accidental one, and color chosen for its effect instead of grabbed at random. That difference is not talent. It is what naming and practicing the tools in Lessons 3 and 4 made possible. Keep both in your portfolio; they are evidence of what you learned to control on purpose.
🔍 PART 2 · CRITIQUE YOUR OWN WORK

Before you call it done, critique your own piece using the same four-step method you used on Kandinsky, Frankenthaler, and Arcay in Lessons 1 and 2. Be your own first critic.

D · Describe

Step back. What shapes, colors, and marks are actually there, with no reference to your intended meaning? Would a stranger see any recognizable object by accident?

A · Analyze

Check your three tools. Is there real rhythm, or does it feel static? Is the balance intentional, or does one side feel dead? Does the color do emotional work, or is it arbitrary?

I · Interpret

Without reading your own mind, could a viewer sense the idea or emotion you set out to express? If not yet, which tool needs to be pushed further?

J · Judge

What one change would most improve it? Usually it is committing further to one tool (bolder color contrast, a clearer rhythm) rather than adding something new.

💭 REVISION CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
You step back and your piece feels flat, like the idea is not coming through. What is the most likely fix?
📝 PART 3 · WRITE YOUR ARTIST STATEMENT

Your artist statement travels with the artwork. It is two or three focused sentences, in your own voice, that show you made deliberate choices, especially important here, since a viewer cannot ask "what is this a picture of?" Type it into the submission text box with your artwork.

A strong statement answers three things:

  • What idea or emotion did you choose, and why does it matter to you?
  • Which tools did you use on purpose, and where? (Name at least two: expressive or geometric marks, rhythm, balance, color emotion.)
  • What surprised you, or what would you do differently next time?
▶  Draft your three sentences in your notebook first, then open this for a sample of the register (do not copy it).
"I made this piece about the tension of waiting for news you cannot control. I used jagged, expressive marks and a high-contrast palette of red against near-black to build tension, and kept the balance asymmetrical so nothing in the composition feels settled. I was surprised how much a single small patch of pale yellow, placed off-center, could suggest a sliver of hope without ever drawing an actual object." Your statement should be this specific, but about your idea and your choices.

✍️ DO NOT FORGET
Every finished artwork carries its artist's name. Sign your piece. A signature is how you say: this is mine, I made it, I stand behind it, even when there is no subject to point to.
