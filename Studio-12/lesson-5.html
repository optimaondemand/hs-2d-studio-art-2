STUDIO 12 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the tools. Now choose your idea, plan the composition, and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE AN IDEA OR EMOTION

Your Studio Project is An Idea Without a Subject: a non-objective artwork that expresses one idea or emotion using only line, shape, and color, with no recognizable object anywhere in the finished work. Choose something specific enough to give you real decisions to make.

🏷️ GOOD CHOICES SHARE THREE TRAITS
A strong idea is specific (not just "happy," but "the relief right after a hard test ends"), it is something you have actually felt so you have real material to draw on, and it suggests a direction for at least one of your three tools (a rhythm, a balance, a color temperature) before you even start sketching.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which kind of idea are you leaning toward?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Even Frankenthaler, whose method looked spontaneous, made choices about canvas size, paint dilution, and color before ever pouring. Plan a few decisions now so your final piece is not a random accident.

  1. Decide your family (from Lesson 3). Will this idea come across better expressively, geometrically, or as a deliberate mix of both? Commit to a starting direction.
  2. Thumbnail three options (about 10 min). Make three small, fast non-objective sketches exploring different arrangements of rhythm, balance, and color for your chosen idea. Do not detail them.
  3. Choose and enlarge. Pick the strongest thumbnail, the one that most clearly carries your idea without any subject. Block in the major shapes lightly on your final surface.
  4. Build with your three tools. Develop rhythm, check balance, and commit to color choices that carry the emotion, from Lesson 4. Work the whole surface, not just one corner.
💡 RESIST THE URGE TO ADD A "THING"
Partway through, many students accidentally start drawing a recognizable shape (a face, a sun, a heart) because it feels safer than trusting pure elements. If that happens, stop and ask what idea you were trying to express, then find a non-objective way (color, rhythm, balance) to express it instead.

🔍 Art Critique · Beyond Reality

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · In VR or on screen. Submit it in the Canvas assignment Beyond Reality (Art Critique).

  • 1Choose: One work from Lessons 1 and 2: Kandinsky's Circles in a Circle, Frankenthaler's Buzzard's Bay, or Atelier Arcay's Composition.
  • 2Describe: What shapes, colors, and marks are there?
  • 3Analyze: How do rhythm, balance, and color work together?
  • 4Interpret: What feeling or idea does the work carry, with no subject to name?
  • 5Judge: Does it succeed? Give one reason.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND NON-OBJECTIVE ART
An AI image generator can produce an abstract-looking image in seconds if you type a prompt like "abstract painting expressing calm." It cannot make the decision this project is actually teaching: choosing, by hand, how much rhythm, what kind of balance, and which specific colors carry a feeling that is yours. An AI output also cannot show a teacher or a viewer the trail of choices behind it, the thing an artist statement asks you to explain. The composing, the color decisions, and the finished work being graded here must be yours.

An Idea Without a Subject

A non-objective artwork, in any media, that expresses one specific idea or emotion using rhythm, balance, and color, with no recognizable object anywhere in the finished piece. This is the one teacher-graded assessment of Studio 12, submitted with a short artist statement.

Start it now and develop it across the week. Full directions, the rubric, and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Studio Project: An Idea Without a Subject. You will finish, refine, and reflect in Lesson 6.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Artists decide before they commit, even artists whose finished work looks spontaneous. A few minutes of thumbnails and a clear idea prevent hours of drifting. Plan your composition, protect your idea, and let the piece grow from deliberate choices, not lucky accidents.
