STUDIO 12 · LESSON 3
Expressive vs. Geometric
Two families of non-objective mark-making, and your hand trying both. Keep loose paper and a pencil or marker nearby. ~50 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Tell the difference between expressive abstraction (gesture, energy, the trace of the body's movement) and geometric abstraction (structure, measured shape, planned order), and produce a quick study in each mode. Most non-objective art leans toward one family or the other, and knowing both gives your Studio Project two real tools instead of one vague impulse.
📚 PART 1 · GESTURE OR STRUCTURE

Every non-objective work makes a choice, even if the artist never states it: does the composition come from the body's movement in the moment, or from a plan decided in advance? Neither is more "advanced." They are two different tools for two different jobs.

Expressive (gestural)Loose, energetic, varied line and mark. Speed and pressure of the hand show through. Edges are soft or broken. Feels discovered, not planned.
Fons Heijnsbroek, 'Beauty & Truth 7.', 2007. Wikimedia Commons, CC0.
GeometricHard-edged, measured shapes: circles, rectangles, triangles. Flat, even color. Relationships between shapes feel decided, not discovered.
Kazimir Malevich (Russian, 1879–1935), Suprematist Composition, 1916. Oil on canvas. Public domain. Wikimedia Commons.
💡 MOST WORKS LEAN, FEW ARE PURE
Frankenthaler's soak-stains (Lesson 2) lean expressive; Arcay's hard-edged shapes lean geometric; Kandinsky's Circles in a Circle actually blends both, gestural color fields crossed by measured straight bands. Your job is not to pick a side forever, but to recognize which tool a moment calls for.
✍️ PART 2 · GUIDED PRACTICE (do this now)

Pick one emotion (anything: excitement, boredom, dread, relief). You will express the same emotion twice, once in each family, on one page divided in half.

  1. Expressive study (4 min). On the left half, work fast. Let your hand's speed and pressure change with the feeling. Do not plan the marks before you make them. Curved, broken, varied line and pressure only.
  2. Geometric study (4 min). On the right half, work slow and deliberate. Choose two or three basic shapes and place them with intention. Decide the size and position of each one before you draw it.
  3. Compare (2 min). Which half feels more like the emotion you picked? Most students find one family fits certain emotions better (dread often reads as jagged and expressive; calm often reads as ordered and geometric), but there is no fixed rule. Notice your own reaction.

🖉️ SKILL BUILDER SKETCH (turn in this week)

One Emotion, Two Families

On one sheet divided in half, express the same chosen emotion once expressively (fast, gestural, varied pressure) and once geometrically (slow, planned, hard-edged shapes). Label each half with the family name and the emotion. This is a completion-graded practice rung, not a finished artwork.

Full directions and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder 1: Expressive/Geometric Study. Photograph your page and upload it, then save a copy to your OneDrive portfolio folder.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Expressive marks show the hand's energy; geometric marks show the mind's plan. Neither is automatically more emotional or more skilled. Choosing between them, or blending them, is the first real decision every non-objective artist makes.
