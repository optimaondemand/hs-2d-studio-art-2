Finding & Vetting Sources

💡 MUSEUM, TEACHING, OR SCHOLARLY: PICK AT LEAST TWO KINDS
A strong set of research notes usually draws on more than one kind of source: a museum record for the hard facts (dates, medium, current location), plus a teaching or scholarly source for context and analysis. Relying on only one source, of any kind, is a thinner foundation than cross-checking two.
✅ PART 2 · THE SOURCE-RELIABILITY CHECK

Before you copy any fact into your research notes, run it through this check. It takes seconds once it is a habit.

SOURCE-RELIABILITY CHECKLIST
✓
Named author or institutionA museum, a scholar, or a named writer stands behind this, not an anonymous post.
✓
On the approved list (or clearly equivalent)A museum collection, a teaching site like Smarthistory or Khan Academy, or a scholarly database.
✓
Specific enough to traceYou can name the exact page, essay, or catalog record, not just "a website I found."
✓
Confirmed by a second source, if it mattersFor any fact you plan to state confidently, a second independent source agrees.
✓
Not resting on an AI summary aloneAn AI tool may have pointed you here, but the fact itself is confirmed in the source, not just repeated from the chat.
TRY IT · Would this source pass the checklist above?
"The Art Institute of Chicago's online collection page for the exact painting, listing its date, medium, and accession number."
"A comment on a social media post that said the artist painted it while traveling."
"A peer-reviewed journal article about the artist, accessed through JSTOR."
📝 VOCABULARY
Approved sourceA museum, teaching, or scholarly source on this course's verified list.
Reliability checkA quick habit of confirming who stands behind a fact before you use it.
TraceableSpecific enough that someone else could find the exact same page or record.
Cross-checkConfirming a fact in a second, independent source.
▶  Before you open your Skill Builder assignment, pick your top candidate artist from Lesson 1's list of three and write down two approved sources you will check first. Then open this.
A strong starting pair is usually one museum source (for hard facts about a specific work) and one teaching or scholarly source (for context on the artist's life and significance). You will use exactly this pattern in Skill Builder 1: Research Notes.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A source is not reliable because it was easy to find. It is reliable because someone with real expertise stands behind it and you can point to exactly where. Build that checking habit now, before the proposal depends on it.
