STUDIO 16 · LESSON 1
What Do You Want to Say?
Every artist you have studied this year answered this question first. Now it is your turn. ~45 min
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
What do YOU want to say, and who has said something like it before?
This Studio has no assigned artist or image, deliberately. You have spent fifteen Studios learning to see, plan, and make with intention. Now you choose the direction, research it responsibly, and write the plan you will build in Studio 17.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK AT THE PATH AHEAD

Before any research happens, look at the whole shape of this Studio. Every artist you have studied since Studio 1, whether a Renaissance draftsman or a living designer, moved through the same four stages to get from a private interest to a finished, defensible piece of work. This Studio walks you through those same four stages, on paper, for your own project.

💬 WONDER FIRST
Think back across this year: Van Gogh's color, Hokusai's pattern, Kahlo's symbolism, Kruger's text-and-image. None of them arrived from nowhere. Each artist studied someone before them, then made a deliberate choice about what to keep and what to do differently. What have you been quietly drawn to all year that you have not yet had the chance to chase?
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Right now, with no pressure to commit, what pulls you most?
📚 PART 2 · NO ARTIST IS A CLEAN SLATE

Every artist works in dialogue: a conversation, across time, with the artists who came before. Picasso answered Cézanne. Kehinde Wiley answers centuries of European court portraiture. Even a completely original idea is usually a new answer to an old question. The word for this connection is influence: a documented, traceable relationship between your project and the artist or movement that shaped your thinking.

This Studio asks you to do formally what artists do informally: name your influence, research it with real rigor, and then say exactly what you intend to do with it. That final move, the "and here is what I will do differently," is your artistic voice starting to take shape on paper before you ever pick up a brush.

THE ONE IDEA OF THIS STUDIO
Study one artist deeply,
then say what you will do that they did not.
Research earns you the right to depart from it. That departure is your Capstone proposal.
ResearchSystematic, sourced investigation of an artist, movement, or technique before you make claims about it.
Primary sourceFirsthand evidence: the artwork itself, the artist's own writing or interview, a museum's catalog record.
Secondary sourceSomeone else's researched account of the artist: a scholarly essay, a museum education page, a documentary.
InfluenceA traceable relationship between your idea and an artist or movement that shaped it.
ThesisOne clear sentence stating what your proposed project will do and why.
ProposalA written plan, before the making begins, that states your thesis, your research, your media, and your steps.
🔍 PART 3 · WHY THIS COMES BEFORE THE CAPSTONE

Studio 17 is your Capstone: one large, self-directed artwork demonstrating everything you have learned this year. A project that size fails most often not from lack of skill, but from lack of planning. Studio 16 exists so that when Studio 17 begins, you are not guessing. You already know your artist, your reasoning, your materials, and your first sketches.

Your project for these two weeks is the Capstone Proposal: research notes on one artist you choose (using verified sources), a page of exploratory sketches, and a written proposal for your Studio 17 project. One submission, three parts, all pointing at the same plan.

▶  Before you read on, name one artist, style, or subject you have wanted to explore all year but have not had the chance to. Then open this.
If a name or idea came to mind quickly, that is likely a strong seed for this Studio. If nothing came to mind, that is normal too: Lesson 3 will walk you through the approved research sources so you can browse toward a direction rather than invent one from nothing. Either way, keep the name or idea; you will need it by Lesson 4.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Artists research. The freedom of an unassigned project is not freedom from study; it is freedom to choose what you study. Pick a direction, investigate it honestly, and let that research earn you the right to say something of your own.
