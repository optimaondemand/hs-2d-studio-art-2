STUDIO 16 · LESSON 4 · SKILL BUILDER B
From Research to Proposal
Turn what you learned about your artist into a thesis, a media plan, and your first sketches. ~55 min
📋 PART 1 · WHAT A PROPOSAL ACTUALLY CONTAINS

A proposal is not a mood board or a vague idea. It is four specific parts, in order, each earning the next.

1
Research summary

Who is your artist, what did you learn, and which sources back it up?

2
Thesis

One clear sentence: what will your project do, and how does it answer or extend your artist?

3
Media plan

What materials or techniques from this year will you use, and why those?

4
Sketches

At least two rough compositions testing the idea on paper before Studio 17 begins.

💬 WONDER FIRST
Notice that the thesis comes after the research, not before it. A proposal is not "I will paint something about identity" written first and researched to match. It is "I studied Amy Sherald's flattened-color portraiture and verified her stated intent through her own interviews, and now I will use a similar flattened palette to paint a portrait of someone in my own community" written only once the research earned it.
✍️ PART 2 · ONE SENTENCE, THREE MOVING PARTS

A thesis for this Studio names your subject, your borrowed device, and your own twist. Fill in the pattern below in your notebook before you write your final version.

MY PROJECTwill [make what / about what subject]
BORROWINGthe [technique or device] that [your artist] used in [specific verified work]
MY TWISTbut I will [what you will do differently, and why]
💡 A THESIS SHOULD BE ARGUABLE, NOT JUST TRUE
"I like Kandinsky's colors" is a preference, not a thesis. "I will use Kandinsky's non-objective color relationships to express a feeling I could not show through a recognizable subject" states a specific plan that a viewer could later judge as done or not done. Aim for the second kind.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Try saying your draft thesis out loud. Which is closest to how it sounds right now?
✎️ SKILL BUILDER 2 (completion-graded)

Thumbnail Sketches: Testing the Idea

Draw at least two small, rough thumbnail sketches (about 20 to 30 minutes total) that test your proposed project's composition. These are not the finished Capstone; they are quick tests of whether your idea works as an image before you commit to it in writing.

Full directions and the submission link are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder 2: Thumbnail Sketches.

📝 VOCABULARY
ThesisOne clear sentence stating what your proposed project will do and why.
Media planThe materials or techniques you intend to use and your reason for choosing them.
Thumbnail sketchA small, quick drawing used to test a composition idea before committing to it.
Artistic voiceThe specific, personal choices that make a project recognizably yours rather than a copy.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Research without a thesis is just notes. A thesis without sketches is just a claim. A proposal is all three together, each part earning the next, which is exactly what Studio 17 will hold you to.
