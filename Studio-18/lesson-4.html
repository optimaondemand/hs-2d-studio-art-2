STUDIO 18 · LESSON 4
The Full Artist Statement
The longer text that carries your whole year, plus the technical work of making your art look its best on screen. ~55 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Write a full artist statement that covers your whole year, not just one piece, and photograph or export your work to a standard clean enough for a professional digital exhibition.
📝 PART 1 · ONE STATEMENT, A WHOLE YEAR

You have written a short artist statement before, for a single Studio Project. The full artist statement for your Final Exhibition is different: it covers your whole curated body of work, not one piece, and it is the single text a stranger reads before looking at anything on your wall. Aim for four solid paragraphs, not a page.

A strong full artist statement covers four things, roughly in this order:

  • Where you started. One or two sentences on what kind of artist you were at the start of the year, before Studio 1.
  • What changed. Name two or three specific skills or ideas that grew across the year (observation, color, composition, a subject you kept returning to). Point to specific Studios as evidence.
  • Why you curated it this way. Explain your short list and your sequence from Lesson 3. Why these pieces, why this order?
  • Where you are now, and where that leaves you. What kind of artist does this body of work show you becoming? You do not need a tidy bow; an honest, specific closing beats a grand one.
💡 SPECIFIC BEATS IMPRESSIVE
"I grew a lot as an artist this year" tells a stranger nothing. "My value range in Studio 1 was timid and flat; by Studio 13 I was pushing full-black core shadows on purpose" tells them everything, and it is verifiable against the actual wall. Name real Studios, real techniques, real choices.
▶  Draft your opening sentence (where you started) in your notebook before you open this.
A workable opening names something concrete, not a vague feeling: "At the start of this year I drew mostly from memory and struggled to get proportions right." Notice it sets up a clear before, which the rest of your statement can then measure change against. Yours will be about your own actual starting point, not this example.
📸 PART 2 · MAKING YOUR WORK LOOK ITS BEST

A great drawing photographed badly reads as a mediocre drawing. Presentation quality is judged on the file a viewer actually sees, so a few minutes of care here protects a whole year of work.

Even, glare-free lightDiffuse daylight or two lamps at 45-degree angles. No single hot spot, no shadow from your own hand or phone.
Straight-on, cropped tightShoot directly above or in front, not at an angle. Crop out the table, wall, and clutter so only the artwork remains.
Export at full resolutionSave or export as a high-resolution JPG or PNG, not a compressed screenshot. Name the file clearly, for example SA2_S18_Title_LastName.jpg.
⚡ TRY IT · SPOT THE PROBLEM
Diagnose each photography problem, then check.
A photo of a charcoal drawing has one bright white blown-out patch where a single lamp reflected directly off the paper.
A photo of a painting is taken from slightly to the side, so the rectangle looks like a trapezoid and the edges are not parallel.
🖉️ SKILL BUILDER SKETCH (turn in this week)

Statement Draft + Two Photographed Works

Write a full draft of your artist statement (all four parts from Part 1). Then photograph or export two works from your curated short list to the professional standard in Part 2: even light, straight-on angle, tight crop, full resolution, clearly named files.

Full directions and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder 2: Statement & Studio Photography. Upload your draft and two image files, then save copies to your OneDrive portfolio folder.

📝 VOCABULARY
Artist statementA written reflection in the artist's voice explaining intent, process, and growth.
DocumentationPhotographing or scanning artwork to a professional standard for presentation.
Diffuse lightSoft, even lighting without a single hot spot or hard shadow.
ResolutionThe detail level of a digital image; higher resolution holds up at larger display sizes.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Words and light are both part of the craft now. A specific, honest statement and a clean, well-lit photograph do for your exhibition what accurate proportion and full value range did for a single drawing: they make the truth of the work visible.
