STUDIO 18 · LESSON 1
A Year, Presented Well
The final Studio. No new technique, no new artist. The subject is everything you have made, and how a stranger will meet it. ~45 min
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
How do you present a year of work so a stranger understands the artist you are?
Every Studio this year taught you to make. This one teaches you to present, the final artistic act most students skip. A viewer who has never met you gets about thirty seconds per piece. This Studio is about earning it.

💬 WONDER FIRST
Picture a stranger walking your digital exhibition with no context, no teacher standing beside them to explain. What would they need on the wall itself, in thirty seconds, to understand who you are as an artist? Hold that answer. This whole Studio builds toward it.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Be honest: if a stranger looked at your best three pieces from this year right now, with no titles and no explanation, would they understand what you were going for?
📚 PART 2 · PRESENTATION IS ITS OWN SKILL

It is tempting to think the art is finished once the last mark is made. It is not. Two artists can make equally strong work and leave completely different impressions, because one learned to present it and the other did not. Presentation is not decoration. It is the last decision an artist makes, and it changes how every earlier decision is understood.

This final Studio has three parts, and none of them is a new drawing technique. You will curate a small, deliberate set from your whole year, learn to write the two kinds of text that travel with an exhibition, a wall label and a full artist statement, and photograph or export your work to a professional standard. Then you will build the real thing: a digital exhibition of your senior body of work.

ExhibitionA curated public presentation of artwork, arranged and labeled with intention.
CurationChoosing which works to include and in what order, so the set makes a clear statement together.
Wall labelThe short card beside a work: title, medium, Studio or date, and sometimes one line of context.
Artist statementA written reflection in the artist's own voice explaining intent, process, and growth.
🔍 PART 3 · WHY THIS STUDIO COMES LAST

Studio 1 asked you to see accurately. Studio 17 asked you to synthesize everything you had learned. This Studio asks something different: look back across all seventeen and decide what the year adds up to, then say it clearly to a stranger.

Your project for these two weeks is the Final Exhibition: a curated body of work with a full artist statement, presented as a professional digital exhibition, a slide deck or web-style gallery. This is not new artwork. It is the finishing move on everything you have already made this year.

▶  In your notebook, name the one piece from this year you are most proud of. Then open this.
Most students can name one instantly, and can also name exactly why: a technique that finally clicked, a subject that mattered, a risk that paid off. That reason is the seed of your artist statement. Keep it. You will need it again in Lesson 4.
