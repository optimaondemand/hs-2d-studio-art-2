STUDIO 18 · LESSON 3
Curate & Sequence
Turn eighteen Studios into one deliberate short list, in order, with labels ready. ~50 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Curate a short list from your full year of work, sequence it so it tells a story of growth, and write a clean wall label for each piece. These three moves are the entire skeleton of your Final Exhibition.
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE, DO NOT COLLECT

Your instinct might be to include everything from all eighteen Studios. Resist it. A body of work with thirty pieces exhausts a viewer; six to ten strong, well-chosen pieces hold their attention. Curation means cutting good work, not just choosing between good and bad.

💡 THREE FILTERS FOR CUTTING THE LIST
Ask of each piece: does it show a skill you want to prove you have (observation, value, color, composition)? Does it show growth compared to earlier work? Does it feel like you, a choice you would defend, not just an assignment you completed? A piece that answers "no" to all three is a candidate to leave out, even if you like it personally.

Aim for six to ten works spanning multiple Studios and at least two different quarters. Include your Capstone from Studio 17; it is your most recent, most synthesized work and belongs in the final exhibition.

📏 PART 2 · ORDER TELLS THE STORY

Once you have your short list, order matters as much as selection. The most common and reliable structure for a student retrospective is chronological with a strong anchor at the end: earliest work first, so the viewer can see where you started, building toward your strongest and most recent piece last.

This is not the only valid order. Some artists sequence by theme instead of date, grouping works that share an idea even if they were made months apart. Whichever you choose, make it deliberate and be ready to explain it in your artist statement.

🏷️ PART 3 · WRITING A WALL LABEL

A wall label is short on purpose. It gives a viewer just enough to orient themselves before they look, then gets out of the way. Every label in your exhibition needs the same four parts, in the same order.

TITLEThe Extraordinary Ordinary
MEDIUMGraphite on paper
STUDIO / DATEStudio 1, Fall 2026
ONE LINE OF CONTEXTFirst observational study of the year
  1. Title. Every piece needs one, even a simple descriptive one. Untitled work reads as unfinished.
  2. Medium. What it is physically made of or with (graphite, charcoal, collage, digital).
  3. Studio and date. Which Studio it came from and roughly when.
  4. One line of context (optional but recommended). A single short phrase, not a paragraph. Save the full explanation for your artist statement in Lesson 4.

🖉️ SKILL BUILDER SKETCH (turn in this week)

Short List + Two Wall Labels

Write your curated short list: six to ten works from across the year, in the order you plan to exhibit them, with one sentence explaining why each piece made the cut. Then write two complete wall labels (title, medium, Studio and date, one line of context) for two of the works on your list.

Full directions and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder 1: Curate & Label. Type or upload your list and labels, then save a copy to your OneDrive portfolio folder.

📝 VOCABULARY

SequenceThe deliberate order works are shown in.
Anchor pieceThe strongest work, often placed last for impact.

ChronologicalOrdered by the date each piece was made.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Curation is subtraction, not addition. Choosing six strong works in a deliberate order says more about you as an artist than displaying thirty ever could. Labels then quietly orient a stranger before they look.
