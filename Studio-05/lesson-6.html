STUDIO 5 · LESSON 6
Refine, Reflect & Present
A strong edition is finished, not just pulled. Critique your series, refine it, and present it well. ~55 min + studio time
🔄 PART 1 · STEP BACK FROM THE SERIES

Lay out every print you pulled from your plate, including your early test prints from Lesson 5. This is the moment to judge the work as a whole series, not just as individual prints.

👀 LOOK AT ALL THE PRINTS TOGETHER
Where does the rhythm feel strongest? Where does registration slip, or ink coverage go patchy? Almost every print series has one or two clear standouts and one or two that reveal a technical lesson. Both are useful; the standouts show what worked, and the weaker ones show exactly what to fix next time.
🔍 PART 2 · CRITIQUE YOUR OWN WORK

Before you call the series done, critique it using the same four-step order you used on Hokusai's wave in Lesson 2: describe, analyze, interpret, judge. Be your own first critic.

D · Describe

Lay your best prints side by side. What does the motif actually look like once repeated? Is the pattern regular or progressive?

A · Analyze

Check your tools. Does the motif repeat with a clear, intentional rhythm? Is the negative space between motifs as considered as the motif itself? Is registration consistent across your best prints?

I · Interpret

What does your finished pattern feel like: calm and orderly, busy and alive, something else? Does that feeling match the natural form that inspired it?

J · Judge

Which print in your series is the strongest, and why? What one change (tighter registration, thinner ink, a cleaner carved edge) would most improve your next pull?

💭 REVISION CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
You lay out your prints and the pattern looks uneven, some motifs crisp, others smeared or crooked. What is the most likely fix?
📝 PART 3 · WRITE YOUR ARTIST STATEMENT

Your artist statement travels with the print series. It is two or three focused sentences, in your own voice, that show you made deliberate choices. Type it into the submission text box with your prints.

A strong statement answers three things:

  • What natural form inspired your motif, and why did you choose it?
  • Which printmaking and pattern tools did you use on purpose, and where? (Name at least two: carving, registration, rhythm, negative space.)
  • What surprised you about repeating your own design, or what would you do differently next time?
▶  Draft your three sentences in your notebook first, then open this for a sample of the register (do not copy it).
"I based my motif on the curl of a fern before it unrolls, because I liked how one small spiral already suggests repeating growth. I carved a foam plate and used a taped corner guide for registration so the spiral would line up the same way across all six prints, and I built a half-drop rhythm so the pattern felt like it was spreading rather than just repeating. I was surprised how much a single crooked print in my test round taught me about how carefully the paper has to be placed every single time." Your statement should be this specific, but about your own motif and choices.

✍️ DO NOT FORGET
Every finished print carries its printmaker's name and its place in the edition. Sign and number your strongest prints (for example, 1/6). That mark is how you say: this is mine, I made it, I stand behind every copy.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

You began Studio 5 asking what changes when an image repeats. Two weeks of carving, inking, and pulling prints gave you a real answer: repetition is not automatic and it is not free. It has to be planned, carved, and aligned on purpose, print after print, the same discipline Hokusai's workshop relied on centuries before you.
