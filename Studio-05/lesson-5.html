STUDIO 5 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the process. Now choose your natural form, plan the block, and begin the series. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE A NATURAL FORM

Your Studio Project is Multiplied: a series of relief prints built around one motif inspired by a natural form. Nature is full of shapes that already repeat, veins on a leaf, scales on a pinecone, ridges on a shell, so it is a natural place to find a motif that already wants to become a pattern.

🏷️ GOOD CHOICES SHARE THREE TRAITS
The form has a clear, simplifiable silhouette (a leaf, a shell spiral, a feather, a wave curl, a flower head), it already suggests repetition in nature (petals, scales, ripples), and it can be reduced to a shape simple enough to carve cleanly. Avoid forms that only look interesting because of fine detail or color; a print plate cannot carry either one well.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which natural form are you leaning toward for your motif?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN BEFORE YOU CARVE

Hokusai's workshop never carved a first attempt straight into good wood. You will not either. Plan the motif, then the block, then test before committing to your final series.

  1. Sketch your motif from the natural form (about 10 min). Simplify your chosen leaf, shell, feather, or wave into a clean, carveable shape, the way you practiced in Lesson 3.
  2. Plan the block. Decide your plate size and whether your rhythm will be regular or progressive. Sketch how the motif will repeat across the plate or across multiple prints.
  3. Carve your plate. Use the process from Lesson 4: transfer the motif, carve away the negative space, keep your lines clean enough to hold up through several pulls.
  4. Test-print before committing. Pull one or two test prints. Check registration, ink coverage, and whether the negative space reads the way you planned. Adjust the plate if needed before starting your real series.
  5. Plan your edition. Decide how many prints your finished series will include, and how you will number them (for example, 1/6 through 6/6).
💡 A TEST PRINT IS NOT WASTED PAPER
Every printmaker pulls test prints before a real edition, called proofs. A proof's whole job is to reveal problems (too much ink, a crooked registration guide, a detail that filled in when carved) while they are still cheap to fix. Expect your first pull to teach you something.

🥽 VR Session · The Pattern Wall

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: The Pattern Wall.

  • 1Before you enter: know your motif and have the terms repetition, rhythm, and registration ready. Enter your VR space and choose any gallery or open Location you like; this activity is platform-agnostic and works solo-first.
  • 2Part 1, Build (about 10 min): using the VR drawing or placement tool, place one simple shape on a wall, then repeat it at least six more times. Try it two ways: once in a straight, regular grid, and once in a half-drop repeat where every other row is offset.
  • 3Part 2, Discuss the multiple (about 8 min): step back and look at the whole wall. Reflect aloud or in writing: what changes about a single shape once it has been repeated a dozen times? Does it stop reading as "a shape" and start reading as "a surface"?
  • 4Part 3, Critique (about 7 min): run a quick critique on your own pattern wall. Describe what is there, analyze the rhythm you built, interpret what feeling it creates, and judge whether the repeat holds together.
  • 5Exit: capture a screenshot of your finished pattern wall and upload it. No screenshot tool? Write two or three sentences describing your straight repeat, your half-drop repeat, and what you judged about the pattern's success.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND PRINTMAKING
An AI image generator can produce a convincing picture of a "repeating pattern" in seconds. It cannot carve a physical block, register a real sheet of paper by hand against that block, or pull an actual physical edition of multiples from it. The graded skill in Multiplied is the hand process itself: carving a motif, inking a plate, aligning the paper the same way each time, and pulling a real, physical series of prints. That process, and the decisions inside it, must be yours.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Every printmaker plans and proofs before they commit to an edition. A carefully chosen natural form, a well-planned block, and one honest test print will save you far more time than they cost. Plan your motif, protect your registration, and let the pattern grow from a process you actually trust.
