STUDIO 17 · LESSON 3
Planning a Large Work
The single biggest reason ambitious projects fail is a plan that skipped this step. ~55 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Turn your Studio 16 proposal into a working plan: thumbnail options for the composition, a set of milestones that break the build into checkable stages, a real materials list, and a time budget that fits the Studio's two weeks. This is the practice rung; your actual milestone plan is due this week.
📚 PART 1 · THUMBNAIL THE WHOLE COMPOSITION FIRST

You have thumbnailed before, in earlier Studios, to test one small composition. A large-scale piece needs the same habit at a bigger stake: solving the whole composition small and fast, before you commit hours to it at full size.

Option ATest where the main mass sits. Does it balance, or does it need a counterweight?
Option BTest a different focal point and a stronger movement path across the whole surface.
Option CIf your piece has multiple panels or sections, test how they read as a set, not just one at a time.
💡 HELPFUL TO KNOW
A thumbnail for a large piece can still be small, a few inches, sketched fast. Its job is to test the big decisions (balance, focal point, movement) cheaply, before those decisions get expensive to change.
✍️ PART 2 · BREAK THE BUILD INTO MILESTONES

A milestone is a planned checkpoint where you can honestly say "this part is done" and look at real, visible progress. Without milestones, a long project feels like one endless unfinished task, which is exactly the feeling that causes students to stall. With them, a big project becomes a series of small, finishable wins.

MILESTONE 1
Composition locked
Thumbnail chosen, final surface prepared, big shapes blocked in.
MILESTONE 2
Structure built
Underlying drawing, layout, or base layer complete across the whole piece.
MILESTONE 3
Development pass
Value, color, texture, or detail developed across most of the surface, not just one corner.
MILESTONE 4
Refine & finish
Final darks, edges, small corrections, signature. Ready to submit.

These four are a starting template. Your own milestones should match your specific medium and proposal, but every plan needs a clear "done" for each stage.

  1. List 3 to 5 milestones in the order you will actually hit them, each with a one-sentence definition of "done."
  2. Assign each one a real deadline inside the Studio's two weeks. Work backward from the final submission date.
  3. List your materials and confirm you have them (or a home-accessible substitute) before Milestone 1.
  4. Build a time budget. Estimate hours per milestone. If the total is more than your available studio time, cut scope now, not in Week 2.

⚡ TRY IT · SPOT THE PLANNING PROBLEM
Read each mini-plan and decide what is wrong with it, then check your answer.
"My milestones are: sketch it, work on it, finish it." No dates, no hour estimates.
A student budgets 40 hours across four milestones for a Studio with about 8 hours of build time total.

Milestone Plan

📝 VOCABULARY
MilestoneA planned checkpoint inside a project that marks real, visible, finishable progress.
Time budgetAn estimate of hours needed for each stage of a project, checked against time actually available.
ScopeThe size and complexity of a project as planned.
ScaleThe size of a work relative to the artist's usual practice and to the idea it carries.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Ambition without a plan usually stalls. Ambition with a milestone map and an honest time budget usually finishes. The plan is not extra work standing between you and the art; it is what makes the art possible to finish at all.
