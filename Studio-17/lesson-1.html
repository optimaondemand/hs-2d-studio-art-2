STUDIO 17 · LESSON 1
One Work, Everything You've Become
Sixteen Studios of looking and making converge into a single large-scale work: your own. ~45 min
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
Can you make one work that shows everything you have become as an artist?
This Studio has no new artist to study and no new technique to learn. It has a single job: take the proposal you researched and wrote in Studio 16 and build it, at scale, with everything your eye and hand have learned since Studio 1. This is the Capstone.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK AT THE ROAD BEHIND YOU

Before you look ahead to the Capstone, look back. Sixteen Studios ago you could not draw an everyday object accurately from memory. Since then you have practiced observation, composition, color, space, pattern, collage, portraiture, abstraction, narrative, design, non-objective work, surrealism, social commentary, experimentation, and research. None of that goes to waste here. It is the raw material of the work you are about to build.

💬 WONDER FIRST
A capstone is not a new assignment bolted onto the end of a course. The word means the stone that finishes an arch, the piece that makes every stone beneath it hold. What would it mean for one artwork to hold the weight of everything you built this year?
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Thinking back across this year, which kind of growth do you feel most in your own hands right now?
📚 PART 2 · WHAT "SYNTHESIS" MEANS HERE

Synthesis means combining separate parts into one connected whole. In Studio 16 you researched an artist, sketched, and wrote a proposal: three separate parts. The Capstone is where those parts become one thing, a finished, large-scale, self-directed artwork that no one is assigning to you piece by piece. You already decided what it is. Your job now is to build it.

"Large-scale" does not require an enormous budget. It means working bigger and more ambitious than your usual Studio project, whatever medium you chose: a bigger sheet, a more complex composition, more panels, more hours. The scale should match the idea in your proposal, not the other way around.

THE ONE IDEA OF THIS STUDIO
The proposal was the plan.
This Studio is the build.
Every lesson from here to Lesson 6 exists to help you plan the build, sustain it, and finish it well.

Two things make a Capstone succeed, and they are what this Studio teaches: a realistic plan for a large work (Lesson 3), and the craftsmanship to sustain quality across a long build instead of losing steam halfway through (Lesson 4).

SynthesisCombining separate skills, ideas, or parts into one unified, connected whole.
CapstoneA culminating work that brings together and demonstrates a body of prior learning.
Self-directedPlanned, paced, and carried out by the artist's own decisions, not step-by-step instructions.
MilestoneA planned checkpoint inside a long project that marks real, visible progress.
🔍 PART 3 · WHY THIS COMES NOW

Every Studio before this one handed you a subject, a medium, and a rubric built around one technique. Studio 17 deliberately hands you almost nothing but your own approved proposal. The final proof of a year of classical training is not that you can follow instructions well; it is that you no longer need many.

▶  Before you read on, open your Studio 16 proposal now and reread it once, start to finish. Then open this.
You just did the single most useful thing you can do before Lesson 2: reconnect with the plan you already trusted enough to submit. Most Capstones drift off course not because the proposal was weak, but because the artist forgot what they originally decided. Keep the proposal open in a tab for the rest of this Studio.
📋 A FIRST LOOK AT CRAFTSMANSHIP, ACROSS A LONG BUILD

Lesson 4 builds this out fully. For now, notice the shape of it: craftsmanship on a long project is not one decision, it is four habits repeated.

1Protect the planReturn to your proposal and milestones before each work session.
2Check in, don't just push forwardPause at each milestone and critique your own progress.
3Revise on purposeChange something because a checkpoint revealed a problem, not by accident.
4Finish, don't just stopThe last 10 percent (edges, signature, final darks) is where craftsmanship shows most.
