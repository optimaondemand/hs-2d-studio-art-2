STUDIO 17 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
Your milestone plan is set. Now the building starts. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CONFIRM YOUR MILESTONE PLAN

Before you pick up a tool, put three things side by side: your Studio 16 proposal, your Lesson 2 critique review of it, and your Lesson 3 milestone plan. Confirm they agree. If Lesson 2 turned up a needed change, this is the moment to lock it into your milestones, not halfway through Milestone 3.

💭 READINESS CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Right now, which part of your Capstone plan feels least settled?
🖌️ PART 2 · START MILESTONE 1

Begin building your Capstone artwork now, in this order.

  1. Prepare your surface or file. Match it exactly to the scale in your proposal and milestone plan.
  2. Lock the composition. Transfer your chosen thumbnail to the full-size surface using whatever method fits your medium (grid, projection, direct drawing).
  3. Work Milestone 1 to completion before moving on. Resist the pull to jump ahead to the "fun part" before the foundation is solid.
  4. Run your first checkpoint (Lesson 4's critique routine) once Milestone 1 is done, even if it feels early. The habit matters more than the timing.
💡 STUDIO TIME IS SELF-PACED
Unlike earlier Studios with a single tight assignment, the Capstone's build happens across your own scheduled sessions this week. Use the hours in your time budget from Lesson 3 as real appointments with the work, not a vague intention.

🔍 Art Critique · Capstone Studio

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · In VR or on screen. Submit it in the Canvas assignment Capstone Studio (Art Critique).

  • 1Choose: Your Capstone as it stands now. Photograph it first.
  • 2Describe: What is there now? Give the medium, subject, and size.
  • 3Analyze: Which Elements and Principles are working hardest?
  • 4Interpret: Does it still match the idea in your proposal?
  • 5Judge: What is strongest? What needs work next?
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND YOUR CAPSTONE
An AI image generator can produce a finished-looking picture in the style of your proposal in seconds. It cannot do what the Capstone is actually assessing: whether you can plan a large project from your own research, sustain your own craftsmanship across many hours, and finish something you decided to make. It also does not know your milestone plan, your materials, or the checkpoints where you already caught and fixed a problem. The planning, the building, the revising, and the finishing are the work being graded here, and they must be yours.
🎨 STUDIO PROJECT (the graded artwork for this Studio)

The Capstone Artwork

A large-scale, self-directed artwork built from your approved Studio 16 proposal, demonstrating both technical skill and conceptual growth across the year. This is the one teacher-graded assessment of Studio 17, submitted with an artist statement connecting your choices to the skills you have learned since Studio 1.

Build it across the week using your milestone plan and checkpoints. Full directions, the rubric, and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Studio Project: The Capstone Artwork. You will finish, refine, and reflect in Lesson 6.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A plan is only useful once you start working it. Confirm it, then begin, and let your milestones and checkpoints carry you across the middle of the build, the part where most ambitious projects quietly lose their nerve.
