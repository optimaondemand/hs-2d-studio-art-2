STUDIO 14 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the toolkit. Now choose your cause, plan the piece, and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE A CAUSE WORTH MAKING

Your Studio Project is A Cause Worth Making: an artwork addressing a contemporary issue or cause that genuinely matters to you, made honestly and school-appropriately. Revisit the issue you named back in Lesson 1. You are committing to it now.

🏷️ GOOD CHOICES SHARE THREE TRAITS
The issue is real to you (you can explain in one honest sentence why it matters), it is specific (a policy, habit, or local issue works better than a huge abstraction), and it is school-appropriate (no depictions of graphic violence, no targeting of a real classmate or staff member, nothing that could not be shown at a school exhibition).
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which category is your cause closest to?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Kruger and Fairey both work from a formula they refined over years. You will build your own version of that discipline in miniature, using every tool from Lessons 3 and 4 before you touch final materials.

  1. Write your one honest sentence. State your cause as plainly as possible. This is your idea, not your slogan yet.
  2. Name your audience. Who specifically needs to see this? People who already agree, or people who need convincing?
  3. Pair up. Pair with one classmate who chose a related cause. Compare your one-sentence issue statements and decide whether your two pieces will hang as a connected pair in the final exhibition, or whether you'll combine them into one shared piece.
  4. Run the fact/opinion check. List your claims and label each Fact or Opinion, the same audit from Lesson 4. Cut anything you cannot back up.
  5. Cut your slogan. Take your sentence through the three-pass slogan-economy process from Lesson 3.
  6. Thumbnail three layouts (about 10 min). Sketch three small, fast options for how image and text will share the space. Do not detail them yet.
  7. Choose and begin. Pick the strongest thumbnail and start building your final piece in your chosen medium.
💡 USE YOUR OWN IMAGES
Draw, paint, photograph, or design your own imagery for this piece. If you photograph a real person, get their permission first and keep the focus on the idea, not on identifying them. Original imagery keeps you in full control of the argument, and it is what the rubric and the copyright rules for this Studio both expect.

🔍 Art Critique · Art & Society

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · In VR or on screen. Submit it in the Canvas assignment Art & Society (Art Critique).

  • 1Choose: One work from Lesson 2: Barbara Kruger's Untitled (I shop therefore I am), or Shepard Fairey's Rise Above or Glass Houses.
  • 2Describe: What words and images are there?
  • 3Analyze: How do contrast, type, and layout make the message read fast?
  • 4Interpret: What is the artist saying about society?
  • 5Judge: Does the design persuade? Judge the design, not the topic.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND YOUR CAUSE ARTWORK
An AI image generator can produce a striking poster-style image about almost any cause in seconds. It cannot verify your facts, choose your audience, or take responsibility for whether your argument is fair, and it cannot know why this particular issue matters to you. Those decisions are the work being graded here. If you use AI at any stage of this project, for brainstorming, for a reference sketch, or for any part of the final image, say so plainly in your artist statement. As Lesson 4 covered, presenting an AI-generated or AI-altered image as your own unedited photograph or drawing is a form of dishonesty this Studio does not accept.
🎨 STUDIO PROJECT (the graded artwork for this Studio)

A Cause Worth Making

An original artwork addressing a contemporary issue or cause important to you, combining image and text with a clear audience and a fair, checkable argument. This is the one teacher-graded assessment of Studio 14, submitted with a short artist statement.

Start it now and develop it across the week. Full directions, the rubric, and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Studio Project: A Cause Worth Making. You will finish, refine, and reflect in Lesson 6.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A cause worth making is planned the way any strong artwork is planned: a clear audience, a checked fact, an edited slogan, and a few honest thumbnails before anything is final. Discipline in the planning is what earns the piece the right to argue.
