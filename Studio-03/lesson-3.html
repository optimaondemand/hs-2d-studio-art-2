STUDIO 3 · LESSON 3
The Color Wheel
The map every colorist carries in their head. Have your color media beside you. ~50 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Read the color wheel: name primary and secondary hues, find complementary and analogous pairs, and split the wheel into warm and cool. These relationships are the vocabulary every color decision is built from.
📚 PART 1 · READ THE WHEEL

The color wheel arranges hues in a circle so their relationships become visible. The three primary colors, red, yellow, and blue, cannot be mixed from other colors. Mix two primaries and you get a secondary: orange, green, or violet. Position on the wheel is what tells you how two colors will behave together.

Primary · red, yellow, blue
Secondary · orange, green, violet
Intermediate · the mixes between them
Directly across = complementary.
Side by side = analogous.
💡 TWO RELATIONSHIPS DO MOST OF THE WORK
Complementary colors sit directly across the wheel (red and green, blue and orange, yellow and violet). Side by side they intensify each other and create energy or tension. Analogous colors sit next to each other (yellow, yellow-orange, orange) and create calm harmony. Van Gogh used both: complementary yellow-and-violet in The Sower, analogous yellows in Sunflowers.
🎨 PART 2 · HARMONIES AND TEMPERATURE

A color harmony is simply a chosen relationship of hues that work together for a purpose. Here are the three you will use most this Studio.

ComplementaryTwo hues opposite on the wheel. High contrast and energy; each makes the other look brighter.
AnalogousThree neighboring hues. Calm and unified; the picture feels of one temperature.
MonochromaticOne hue at many values and intensities. Quiet, focused, and easy to keep unified.

The wheel also splits into warm and cool. Warm hues tend to advance toward the viewer and feel energetic; cool hues tend to recede and feel calm or distant. Temperature is one of your fastest tools for setting mood.

WarmReds, oranges, yellows. Advance, energize, warm the scene.
CoolGreens, blues, violets. Recede, calm, cool the scene.
⚡ TRY IT · NAME THE RELATIONSHIP
Use the wheel above. For each pairing, decide the relationship, then check.
Blue and orange placed right next to each other in a painting.
A scene built only from red, orange, and yellow-orange.
Yellow, yellow-green, and green used together.
🖉️ SKILL BUILDER SKETCH (turn in this week)

Your Own Color Wheel + Harmony Swatches

On one sheet, mix and paint (or color) a 12-part color wheel from just the three primaries. Then, below it, make three small swatch strips that clearly show a complementary pair, an analogous set of three, and a monochromatic set of three. Label each.

This is a completion-graded practice rung, not a finished artwork. Full directions and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder 1: Color Wheel & Harmonies. Photograph your page, upload it, and save a copy to your OneDrive portfolio folder.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

The color wheel is not decoration; it is a map of relationships. Once you can see complementary, analogous, and warm and cool at a glance, you stop guessing at color and start choosing it. That is the difference between filling in shapes and painting on purpose.
