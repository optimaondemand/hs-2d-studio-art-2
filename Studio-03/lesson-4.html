STUDIO 3 · LESSON 4
Color & Mood
The wheel tells you what colors relate. This lesson tells you what they feel like. ~55 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Change a color's value and intensity to shift its feeling, use temperature to set mood, and choose symbolic color thoughtfully. This is the lesson where color stops being labels and starts being expression.
📚 PART 1 · VALUE AND INTENSITY CHANGE THE FEELING

A hue is only the beginning. The same red can be a soft blush or a heavy maroon depending on its value, and it can be a screaming fire-engine red or a dusty brick depending on its intensity. Those two dials do most of the mood-setting.

Value is how light or dark a color is. High-value colors (tints) tend to feel light, airy, and gentle; low-value colors (shades) feel heavy, serious, or somber. Here is one hue moved across the value range:

One hue, light tint to dark shade. Light values lift the mood; dark values weigh it down.

Intensity (saturation) is how pure or dulled a color is. Pure high-intensity color feels bold, urgent, or festive; a dulled low-intensity color feels quiet, tired, or melancholy. Artists lower intensity by mixing in a little of the complement or a neutral. Here is one hue from full intensity down to nearly gray:

One hue, high intensity to low. Bright feels bold; dulled feels quiet.

💡 THE FASTEST WAY TO KILL A MOOD BY ACCIDENT
A "cheerful" scene made only of dark, dull colors will not read as cheerful, no matter how warm the hues are. Decide your mood first, then set hue, value, and intensity to serve it.
🎨 PART 2 · ONE SUBJECT, THREE MOODS

Here is the exact move your Studio Project asks for. One subject, a single tree, shown three ways. The shapes stay the same. Only hue, value, intensity, and temperature change, and the feeling changes completely.

JoyfulWarm, high-intensity, mid-to-light value. Reads as sunlit and alive.
CalmCool, mid-intensity, light-to-mid value. Reads as quiet and still.
MournfulDark, low-intensity, cool-leaning. Reads as heavy and somber.

Symbolic color can add another layer. In many contexts red suggests passion or danger, blue suggests calm or sadness, and green suggests growth. But these meanings are not universal: they shift across cultures and situations. Use symbolic color as a deliberate choice you can explain, not as a rule you obey. The most reliable mood tool is still the relationship between hue, value, intensity, and temperature.

⚡ TRY IT · BUILD THE MOOD
Answer each from what you just learned. Commit, then check.
You want a scene to feel tired and melancholy without changing the hues. What do you lower?
To make a landscape feel calm and a little distant, you would lean the palette toward which temperature?
A soft pink and a heavy maroon are the same hue. What is different between them?
▶  In your notebook, name your subject for the project and jot one word for each of the three moods you might build, before you open this.
Strong mood words are specific and different from one another: for example "festive," "lonely," and "uneasy" pull in three clear directions, while "happy," "good," and "nice" barely differ. The bigger the emotional distance between your three moods, the more clearly your color choices will read. You will lock these in during Lesson 5.
🖉️ SKILL BUILDER SKETCH (turn in this week)

Same Shape, Three Moods

Draw one simple shape or object three times (three small boxes on one sheet). Color each version to express a different mood, changing hue, value, intensity, and temperature but keeping the shape identical. Under each, write the one mood word you were after.

This is a completion-graded practice rung. Full directions and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder 2: Same Shape, Three Moods. Photograph your page, upload it, and save a copy to your OneDrive portfolio folder.

📝 VOCABULARY
HueThe name of a color: red, blue, yellow-green.
ValueHow light or dark a color is.
TintA hue lightened, usually toward white.
ShadeA hue darkened, usually toward black.
Intensity / saturationHow pure or dulled a color is.
TemperatureWhether a color reads as warm or cool.
Symbolic colorColor used for its associated meaning, which can vary by culture.
MoodThe overall feeling a work gives the viewer.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Hue names the color; value and intensity give it its feeling. Decide the mood first, then set every dial to serve it. Do that on one subject three times, and you have proven that color, not the subject, is what speaks.
