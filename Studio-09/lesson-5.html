STUDIO 9 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have two roads to abstraction. Now choose your subject, plan the journey, and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE A SUBJECT WORTH TRANSFORMING

Your Studio Project is From Study to Stylization: an observational drawing of one object or natural form, transformed into an original stylized or abstract composition, with the stages of that transformation visible. Choose a subject that gives you something worth simplifying.

🏷️ GOOD CHOICES SHARE THREE TRAITS
It has a clear, simple silhouette (a leaf, a shell, a flower, a piece of fruit, a simple tool), it has at least one interesting curve, texture, or repeated pattern worth exaggerating, and it is something you can observe directly, not from a photo alone. Natural forms tend to reward this project especially well, the way they rewarded O'Keeffe.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which direction are you leaning for your transformation?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN THE JOURNEY BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Because your grade depends on showing the progression, plan the stages now rather than discovering them by accident later.

  1. Observe and draw (about 15 min). Draw your chosen subject from direct observation, as accurately as you can. This is your anchor drawing; every later stage will be judged against how clearly it grew from this one.
  2. Decide your path. Will you climb the abstraction ladder (simplify, then stylize), lean on cropping and magnification, or combine both? Write your plan in one sentence before you draw further.
  3. Sketch two or three transitional stages (about 20 min) that show the decisions along the way. Keep every stage, even rough ones; they are part of what you submit.
  4. Push to your final stylized or abstract composition. This is the piece that goes furthest from the anchor drawing, and it should still be traceable back to it through the stages you kept.
💡 KEEP EVERY STAGE, EVEN THE MESSY ONES
Do not throw away your early drawings once you move on. The rubric grades the progression, not just the final image. A strong final piece with no visible process behind it earns less credit than a clear four-stage journey, even if the final image is simpler.

🥽 VR Session · The Abstraction Path

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: The Abstraction Path.

  • 1Before you enter: sketch or photograph one small object nearby (your own choice, separate from your Studio Project subject if you like). Enter your VR space; you may choose any Location you like.
  • 2Stage 1 (about 5 min): using the VR drawing or note tool, record your object realistically, exactly as observed.
  • 3Stage 2 (about 7 min): simplify or stylize that same object, one deliberate change at a time, the way you practiced in Lesson 3.
  • 4Stage 3 (about 7 min): push it to non-representational: keep only shape and color, with no obvious link back to the object on its own.
  • 5Reflect (about 6 min): answer aloud or in writing: "Which stage was the hardest decision to make, and why?"
  • 6Exit: capture a screenshot of your three stages and upload it. No screenshot tool? Write two or three sentences describing each stage.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND ABSTRACTION FROM OBSERVATION
An AI image generator can produce an image described as "abstract style" or "in the style of Georgia O'Keeffe" in seconds. It cannot do what this project asks: start from an object you actually observed, drawn by your own hand, and make a traceable series of decisions that move it toward abstraction. An AI generating a finished "abstract" image has no observational drawing underneath it to build from, and it did not choose to simplify, exaggerate, or crop any specific shape you drew. The progression itself, not just a final image, is what is being graded here, and every stage of it must be yours.
🎨 STUDIO PROJECT (the graded artwork for this Studio)

From Study to Stylization

An observational drawing of your chosen object or natural form, transformed through visible stages into an original stylized or abstract composition. This is the one teacher-graded assessment of Studio 9, submitted with your anchor drawing, your transitional stages, your final piece, and a short artist statement.

Start it now and develop it across the week. Full directions, the rubric, and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Studio Project: From Study to Stylization. You will finish, refine, and reflect in Lesson 6.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A strong transformation is never a leap. It is a series of visible, deliberate steps away from an accurate starting point. Plan the steps, keep every stage, and let your final piece be the destination of a journey a viewer can retrace.
