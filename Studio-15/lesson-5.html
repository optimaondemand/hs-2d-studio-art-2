STUDIO 15 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the method. Now scale it up into a real series of experimental works. ~55 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · DESIGN YOUR SERIES OF EXPERIMENTS

Your Studio Project is Risk Log: A Series of Experimental Works. You will produce a small series (at least three finished pieces) that each combine two or more materials or techniques learned this year, plus a written log of what was tried and an artist statement on what was risked and learned. This is not one careful masterpiece; it is a body of honest, documented risk.

🏷️ A STRONG SERIES HAS FOUR TRAITS
It combines at least two techniques or media from different Studios this year in each piece, it is built from a documented process (you can point to the trials that led to each finished piece), it takes at least one real risk where you genuinely did not know the outcome in advance, and at least one piece pushes into a dimensional or fiber-based technique, for example built-up modeling paste, torn fabric, or stitched thread layered into the surface. A series where every piece looks safe and predictable has not met the assignment.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which combination from this year are you most drawn to testing for your series?
🖌️ PART 2 · FROM SCRAPS TO A SERIES

Your Lesson 3 and Lesson 4 skill builders already ran small trials on scraps. Now you scale the most promising trials up into finished pieces.

  1. Review your logs (10 min). Look back at every trial from Lessons 3 and 4. Which outcomes genuinely surprised you or seemed worth developing further?
  2. Choose two or three directions (10 min). Pick the strongest trials, not necessarily the prettiest ones, and plan one finished piece around each.
  3. Set your controls for the series (10 min). Decide what will stay consistent across your series, for example the same surface size or the same base material, so the series reads as connected trials rather than unrelated pieces.
  4. Build and log as you go. As you make each finished piece, keep noting what you changed and what happened. The log is part of the submission, not an afterthought.
💡 A FAILED PIECE CAN STILL EARN A STRONG GRADE
The rubric for this Studio rewards documented, intentional risk, not a guaranteed beautiful result. A piece that did not turn out the way you hoped, honestly described in your log and statement, demonstrates exactly the habit this Studio teaches. A safe piece with no real risk earns less credit than an honest failure.

🥽 VR Session · The Materials Lab

Week 2 · about 20 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: The Materials Lab.

  • 1Before you enter: have your variable-and-control vocabulary from Lesson 3 ready. Enter your VR space; you may choose any Location you like.
  • 2Run three quick digital experiments (about 12 min): using the VR drawing or building tool, try three fast tests, for example, the same shape drawn with three different line weights, three different color combinations on the same form, or three arrangements of the same objects. Change one variable each time.
  • 3Log outcomes (about 5 min): for each of the three, note in writing or a short voice memo what you changed and what you noticed. Which result surprised you most?
  • 4Exit: capture a screenshot of your three quick experiments or your notes, and upload it. No screenshot tool? Write two or three sentences describing what you tried.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND MATERIAL EXPERIMENTATION
An AI image generator can produce a picture that looks like torn paper, dripped ink, or melted wax in seconds, and it can even describe what "might happen" if two materials are combined. What it cannot do is combine your actual paint with your actual paper and show you the real result, because that result depends on physical materials behaving in the physical world, humidity, absorbency, drying time, none of which the AI is testing. It also cannot take the real risk this Studio is grading: committing to an unknown outcome and living with what happens. The trials, the log, and the finished series must be yours.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A series of experiments is not a series of accidents. It is scraps and small trials, deliberately reviewed and scaled up, with the log kept honest the whole way. That is how a real risk becomes a finished body of work.
