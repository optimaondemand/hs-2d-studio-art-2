STUDIO 1 · LESSON 3
Line & Contour
Your first hands-on tools for recording what you see. Keep a pencil and paper beside you. ~50 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Draw the true edges of an object using contour line, train pure observation with blind contour, and use negative space to check your accuracy. These three tools all serve one goal: getting your hand to record your eye, not your memory.
📚 PART 1 · THREE WAYS TO USE LINE

A contour is not an outline you invent; it is the real edge where a form turns away from you, or where one surface meets another. Line is the most honest tool in drawing, because it shows exactly where your eye actually went.

Contour LineOne steady, continuous line that follows every outer and inner edge of the object. Move your eye and your pencil at the same slow speed.
Hans Holbein the Younger, William Reskimer, c. 1533. Wikimedia Commons, public domain.
Blind ContourThe same slow line, but you never look at your paper. The result looks wobbly and wrong, and that is the point: it forces your eye onto the object.
Jolene Davis Cabe, Blind Contour Drawing of a Young Man's Face. Wikimedia Commons, public domain (CC0).
Negative SpaceInstead of the object, draw the shapes of the empty space around it. In Tohaku's screen almost half the surface is bare mist, yet those empty shapes are what give the pines their place and scale. Drawing the gaps, not the thing, is often the fastest way to catch a proportion error.
Hasegawa Tohaku, Pine Trees (Shorin-zu byobu), c. 1595. Wikimedia Commons, public domain.
💡 WHY BLIND CONTOUR LOOKS "BAD" ON PURPOSE
A blind contour drawing is never meant to be accurate or pretty. It is an eye-training exercise. When you cannot check the paper, your brain stops drawing the symbol and starts following the real edge. Every artist keeps this drill in their toolkit, no matter how advanced.
✍️ PART 2 · GUIDED PRACTICE (do this now)

Grab your non-dominant hand as your model. It is free, it holds still, and it is genuinely hard to draw from memory. Work through all three warm-ups on one page.

  1. Blind contour (2 min). Look only at your hand. Place your pencil down and, without lifting it or looking at the paper, trace every edge slowly. Move your eye and pencil together. Do not peek.
  2. Modified contour (3 min). Now you may glance at the paper occasionally to reposition, but spend most of your time looking at the hand. Draw the outer edge and the big interior creases as one connected line.
  3. Negative space check (3 min). Find one clear gap, for example the space between two fingers, and draw just that empty shape. Ask: is it a tall thin triangle or a short wide one? Drawing the gap accurately fixes the fingers around it.
⚡ TRY IT · PICK THE RIGHT TOOL
For each situation, decide which observation tool solves it best. Commit, then check.
Your drawing of a chair looks off, but you cannot tell why. The parts seem right individually.
You keep drawing the "idea" of an eye instead of the real one in front of you.
🖉️ SKILL BUILDER SKETCH (turn in this week)

Three-Part Contour Warm-Up

On one sheet, complete a blind contour, a modified contour, and one negative-space study of a single object of your choice (your hand, a shoe, a pair of scissors). Label each part. This is a completion-graded practice rung, not a finished artwork. Focus on effort and honest looking.

Full directions and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Skill Builder 1: Contour Warm-Up. Photograph your page and upload it, then save a copy to your OneDrive portfolio folder.

📝 VOCABULARY
Contour lineA line that follows the real outer and inner edges of a form, without shading.
Blind contourDrawing an edge while looking only at the object, never at the paper.
Negative spaceThe shape of the empty area around and between objects.
Positive spaceThe area the object itself occupies.
Continuous lineA single unbroken line drawn without lifting the pencil.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Line records exactly where your eye went. Blind contour trains the eye, continuous contour follows the real edge, and negative space checks your accuracy. None of them require talent. They require slowing down and trusting what is actually there.
