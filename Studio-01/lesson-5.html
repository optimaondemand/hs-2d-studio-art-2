STUDIO 1 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the tools. Now choose your subject, plan the drawing, and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE A MEANINGFUL OBJECT

Your Studio Project is The Extraordinary Ordinary: a refined observational drawing, in graphite or charcoal, of one everyday object that means something to you. Meaning matters because you will look at this object longer than you have ever looked at anything. Choose one that rewards the attention.

🏷️ GOOD CHOICES SHARE THREE TRAITS
It holds still (no pets), it has visible form and texture to reward shading (a worn shoe, a house key, a seashell, an instrument, a pair of glasses), and it carries a story for you. Avoid perfectly smooth, featureless objects; they give value nothing to do.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which kind of object are you leaning toward?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Masters plan. Before Leonardo painted, he filled pages with studies. You will do a smaller version: a set of quick thumbnail sketches to solve composition before you touch the final paper.

  1. Set your light. Place your object under a single, steady light source (a window or one lamp). A clear light direction is what makes value possible. Keep it the same for two weeks.
  2. Thumbnail three views (about 10 min). Make three small, fast sketches from different angles and placements. Do not detail them. You are testing which composition is strongest.
  3. Choose and enlarge. Pick the strongest thumbnail. Use sighting from Lesson 4 to block in the big proportions lightly on your good paper.
  4. Contour, then value. Lay in the true contours from Lesson 3, then build value through the five light zones. Work from your object, not your memory, the entire time.
💡 WORK LIGHT TO DARK
In graphite and charcoal you can always go darker, but lifting out dark is hard. Establish your lightest midtones first, then deepen the core and cast shadows last. Save your brightest highlights as untouched paper.

🥽 VR Session · The Observation Gallery

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · Works for a solo student; a live class runs it unchanged. This is a completion-graded activity; full submission steps are in the Canvas assignment VR Session: The Observation Gallery.

  • 1Before you enter: have your vocabulary and the four DAIJ steps ready. Enter your VR space and load the Studio 1 gallery snapshot from Canvas. You may choose any gallery Location you like.
  • 2Critique (about 10 min): move close to one drawing that pulls your eye. Using your VR note tool, place short notes running DAIJ on it. Which observation tools (contour, proportion, value) did the artist rely on?
  • 3Reflect (about 8 min): answer aloud or in writing: "What did this artist notice that a casual viewer would miss?" If you are in a live session, share and respond to one classmate.
  • 4Make (about 7 min): use the VR drawing tool to sketch a quick blind contour of any object in the space. Same drill as Lesson 3, new tool.
  • 5Exit: capture a screenshot of your notes or sketch and upload it. No screenshot tool? Write two or three sentences describing what you did.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND OBSERVATIONAL DRAWING
An AI image generator can produce a photorealistic picture of "a worn leather shoe" in seconds. It cannot do the thing this project is teaching: the act of looking. The whole point of The Extraordinary Ordinary is what happens in your eye and hand over two weeks of direct observation. An AI can also not sight the real proportions of the object on your desk or record how the light actually falls on it today. The looking, the decisions, and the drawing are the work being graded here, and they must be yours.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Artists decide before they commit. A few minutes of thumbnails and a clear light source prevent hours of frustration. Plan your composition, protect your light, and let the drawing grow from careful looking, not lucky guessing.
