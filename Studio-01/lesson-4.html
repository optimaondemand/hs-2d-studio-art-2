STUDIO 1 · LESSON 4
Proportion & Value
Get the sizes right, then turn flat line into form that looks solid enough to pick up. ~55 min
🎯 WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
Measure real size relationships by sighting, build a controlled value scale, and use value to turn a flat shape into a three-dimensional form. Line gives you the edges; value gives you the weight.
📏 PART 1 · GET THE SIZES RIGHT (SIGHTING)

Proportion is the size relationship between one part of an object and another. Most drawings that "look wrong" are not badly drawn; they are badly measured. Chuck Close solved this with a grid. You will use the artist's older, portable version: sighting with a pencil.

  1. Hold your pencil at arm's length, elbow locked, and line the tip up with the top of your object. Slide your thumb down the pencil to mark where the bottom of one part falls. That distance is now your unit of measure.
  2. Compare. Keeping your thumb fixed, move the pencil to another part of the object. Ask: is this part one unit tall, half a unit, two units? "The handle is about half as tall as the cup."
  3. Transfer those ratios to your paper. You are not copying inches; you are copying relationships. As long as the ratios match, the drawing reads as accurate at any size.
💡 THE ONE RULE OF SIGHTING
Lock your elbow and keep the pencil the same distance from your eye every time, or your unit changes and the measurements lie. Compare part to part, never part to a ruler. Drawing is about relationships, not real-world inches.
🌑 PART 2 · VALUE BUILDS FORM

Value is how light or dark a surface is. It is the single most powerful tool for making flat paper look three-dimensional. First, build control with a value scale: even steps from your lightest light to your darkest dark.

A 7-step value scale, lightest to darkest. You will draw and shade your own by hand.

Now put value to work. A flat circle stays a coin until value turns it into a sphere. Every rounded form has the same five zones of light:

The five zones: the highlight (brightest, where light hits directly), the midtone, the core shadow (the darkest band, where the form turns away), reflected light (a faint lift on the shadow edge), and the cast shadow on the surface below. Find one light source and place these in order, and any object becomes solid.

⚡ TRY IT · READ THE LIGHT
Answer each from what you just learned. Commit, then check.
On a sphere lit from the upper left, which zone is the darkest part of the ball itself?
A student's drawn mug has a handle nearly as tall as the whole cup. Which skill fixes this?
A circle looks like a flat coin. What turns it into a believable ball?
▶  In your notebook, shade a small circle into a sphere before you open this. Then check your zones.
Compare against the five zones: is your highlight a clean untouched spot near the light? Is your core shadow a curved band (not at the very edge, but just inside it)? Did you leave a sliver of reflected light along the shadow edge? Is your cast shadow darkest right where the ball meets the surface? Fixing those four checks turns a gray blob into a sphere.

📝 VOCABULARY
ProportionThe size relationship between the parts of an object.
SightingMeasuring size relationships with a pencil held at arm's length.
ValueHow light or dark a surface is.
Value scaleAn even, stepped range from lightest to darkest.
FormThe illusion of a solid, three-dimensional object.
Core shadowThe dark band where a form turns away from the light.
Cast shadowThe shadow an object throws onto a nearby surface.
HighlightThe brightest spot, where light hits the form most directly.
