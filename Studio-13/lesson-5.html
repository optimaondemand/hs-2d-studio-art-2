STUDIO 13 · LESSON 5 · WEEK 2 BEGINS
Plan & Create
You have the devices and the rendering skill. Now build your dream world and begin. ~60 min + studio time
📌 PART 1 · CHOOSE YOUR CENTRAL IDEA

Your Studio Project is Dream Worlds: a surreal artwork combining realistically rendered observation with imagination, in which real, well-drawn elements sit in an impossible relationship. Return to your Lesson 1 Impossible List and your Lesson 3 and 4 thumbnails. Choose the one idea that feels most specific to you, not the most generic "weird" idea.

🏷️ STRONG DREAM IDEAS SHARE THREE TRAITS
They break one rule clearly (one primary device: dislocation, scale, metamorphosis, or dream logic), they use real observed objects or spaces as their raw material (so your rendering skill has something true to stand on), and they connect to something true about you, a real feeling, memory, or question, even if the image itself is invented.
💭 PLANNING CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which primary device is your idea leaning on most?
🖌️ PART 2 · PLAN BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Dalí filled sketchbooks testing dream images before committing to a final canvas. Do the same in miniature.

  1. Write your one-sentence idea. "An ordinary [object] is [impossible thing], in a setting that is otherwise completely believable." If you cannot say it in one sentence, it is not focused enough yet.
  2. Decide your single light source. Lock in the direction from Lesson 4 and commit to it for the whole piece.
  3. Thumbnail two compositions (about 10 min). Sketch two small, fast layouts testing where the impossible element sits in the frame and how the eye enters the scene.
  4. Choose and block in. Pick the stronger thumbnail. Sight the proportions of every object, real or impossible, and lightly block in the whole composition on your good paper.
💡 ANCHOR THE IMPOSSIBLE WITH THE ORDINARY
The most convincing surreal images almost always include at least one completely ordinary, carefully observed element (a real horizon, a real chair, a real hand) next to the impossible one. That ordinary anchor is what lets a viewer trust your rendering enough to believe the impossible part beside it.

🔍 Art Critique · Dream Worlds

Week 2 · about 25 minutes · In VR or on screen. Submit it in the Canvas assignment Dream Worlds (Art Critique).

  • 1Choose: One work from Lesson 2: Dalí's The Persistence of Memory, Magritte's Les valeurs personnelles, or Varo's Dolor reumático or Paraíso de los gatos.
  • 2Describe: What is shown? List what you see before you explain it.
  • 3Analyze: Which device does the most work: dislocation, impossible scale, or metamorphosis?
  • 4Interpret: What can this dream say that a realistic scene could not?
  • 5Judge: Does it succeed? Give one reason.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND SURREAL IMAGERY
An AI image generator is very good at producing "weird" or dreamlike combinations instantly, because it can remix countless existing images without ever deciding what any of it means. What it cannot do is start from a real object you have observed, choose one specific device on purpose, and render it using proportion and value you measured and built yourself, in service of an idea that is actually yours. This project is graded on the choices behind your image, the device you committed to and the truth you were trying to tell, and on the rendering skill built through direct practice this Studio. Both must be your own work.
🎨 STUDIO PROJECT (the graded artwork for this Studio)

Dream Worlds

A surreal artwork combining realistically rendered elements in an impossible relationship, using one clear device (dislocation, impossible scale, metamorphosis, or dream logic), a single consistent light source, and a full value range. This is the one teacher-graded assessment of Studio 13, submitted with a short artist statement.

Start it now and develop it across the week. Full directions, the rubric, and submission steps are in the Canvas assignment Studio Project: Dream Worlds. You will finish, refine, and reflect in Lesson 6.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A dream world only convinces when it is planned like a real one: one light source, one clear device, real observation underneath the invention. Commit to your idea now, and let the drawing grow from a decision, not a guess.
