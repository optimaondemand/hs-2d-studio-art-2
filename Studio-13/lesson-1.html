STUDIO 13 · LESSON 1
What Truths Live in Dreams?
A key the size of a person. A clock draped like cloth. Real things, impossible relationships. ~45 min
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
What truths can you only tell through a dream?
Every Studio so far has asked you to render the world convincingly. This Studio keeps every rendering skill you built and turns it toward something that never existed. Surrealism is not sloppy fantasy; it is precise craftsmanship in service of the impossible.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

Before any names or theory, look at the scene below for a full minute. Everything in it is drawn the way you have trained all year: real edges, real light, real space. And yet nothing here should be possible.

ordinary key
the same key, impossible scale
a clock, bent like cloth

Original concept diagram, built for this lesson. Two identical objects, two different scales, one impossible ground. This single move, changing scale or material against everything you know to be true, is the surrealist's basic tool.

Notice what did not happen. The key is not glowing, sparkling, or cartoon-styled. It is rendered with the same careful light and shadow logic as anything you have drawn from observation this year. The dislocation is entirely in the relationship, a huge key beside a normal one, a clock with the softness of fabric, not in the drawing style. That is the surrealist method: draw the impossible as convincingly as you would draw what is real.

💬 WONDER FIRST
If an artist can draw anything, why would they spend hours perfecting the realistic shading on an object that could not exist? What does convincing rendering add to an impossible image that a loose, cartoonish sketch of the same idea would not? Hold your answer. You will test it on your own artwork by the end of this Studio.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Think of an actual dream you remember. What made it strange was usually not that everything was strange. Which is closer to what you remember?
📚 PART 2 · SURREALISM, DEFINED

Surrealism began in the 1920s among writers and artists who wanted to paint what the conscious, rule-following mind edits out: the logic of dreams, free association, and the unconscious. They believed dreams told a truth daylight logic could not reach, and that painting it honestly meant rendering it as solidly as anything real.

This Studio features three artists who each found a different route into that same unconscious territory: a painter of hyper-real, melting objects; a painter of ordinary things in quietly wrong relationships; and a painter of transformation and myth. Their work is still under copyright, so you will meet all three through description and a museum link, not an image reproduced here.

THE ONE IDEA OF THIS STUDIO
Render it real.
Make it impossible.
Surrealism does not weaken your craft to make room for imagination. It demands your strongest observational skills in service of something that has never existed.

Four devices do most of that work, and you will study and practice each one across this Studio. Meet them by name now.

DislocationPlacing an ordinary object in a setting or context where it does not belong.
Impossible scaleMaking an object dramatically larger or smaller than it should be relative to its surroundings.
MetamorphosisOne form transforming into, or fusing with, another within a single image.
Dream logicAn internal consistency that feels emotionally true even though it defies waking-world rules.
🔍 PART 3 · WHY THIS COMES NOW

By this point in 2D Studio Art 2 you have built real observational and rendering skill: proportion, value and form, space, color, texture. Studio 13 asks you to spend all of it on invention. Nothing here works unless the impossible parts are drawn as carefully as the possible ones. A weakly rendered dream image just looks like a mistake; a carefully rendered one looks like a truth you were not expecting.

Your project for these two weeks is called Dream Worlds. You will build one surreal artwork that combines realistically rendered elements in an impossible relationship, backed by an artist statement explaining the truth you were trying to tell.

▶  Before you read on, picture one ordinary object from your own life. Name one impossible thing you could do to it. Then open this.
Almost anyone can generate one instantly: make it enormous, make it liquid, put it somewhere it could never survive, combine it with something living. That instant idea is not a joke, it is raw material. Surrealists trusted the first strange image that came to mind and then spent real craft making it believable. Keep your object in mind; it may become your project's seed.
